
# 🔥Daniel Moses Alemoh Responds to Obidi's Article on "The World as I See It: A Brief Philosophical Reflection from the Standpoint of the Theory of Entropicity (ToE)""

Daniel-Moses-Alemoh-Responds-to-Obidi's-Article-on-"The-World-as-I-See-It-A-Brief-Philosophical-Reflection-from-the-Standpoint-of-the-Theory-of-Entropicity-(ToE)"".md

There are scientific articles that explain an idea, and then there are pieces that invite you to step inside an idea and see the world through its eyes.



This is the latter.



What makes this reflection particularly compelling is that Obidi is not merely presenting another physical model. He is asking a much deeper question: 



**what if our description of reality has been looking at the manifestations of the universe rather than its underlying substrate?**



The language of entropy, information, geometry and emergence is used here not simply as technical vocabulary, but as a philosophical lens through which the familiar world begins to look strangely unfamiliar.



**“We are temporary, but not trivial. We are finite, but not disconnected. We are entropic, but not meaningless.”**



That passage, for me, captures the spirit of the entire reflection.



There is also something remarkably personal about this piece. Beneath the equations and physical propositions is the portrait of a thinker who has spent years pursuing a question that apparently refused to let him go. 



**And that is perhaps where every ambitious theory begins—not with certainty, but with a persistent conviction that there is a deeper pattern worth pursuing.**



What I find most intriguing is the willingness to leave the theory unfinished.

To say, in effect: ***This is what I see. Examine it. Challenge it. Refine it. And if it is wrong, correct it.***



That is a far more interesting invitation than simply asking the reader to believe.



Whether the Theory of Entropicity (ToE) ultimately establishes itself as a viable physical theory will depend, as it must, on mathematics, derivations, empirical predictions and experimental scrutiny. 



But as a philosophical reflection, this piece succeeds in something important: 

**it makes you pause and ask whether “matter,” “space,” “time,” and even “self” are really as fundamental as our everyday experience makes them seem.**



**This is not merely an article about physics.**



**It is an invitation to look at the universe from somewhere else.**



**And once you accept the invitation, you may find that the familiar world looks just a little less familiar.**
