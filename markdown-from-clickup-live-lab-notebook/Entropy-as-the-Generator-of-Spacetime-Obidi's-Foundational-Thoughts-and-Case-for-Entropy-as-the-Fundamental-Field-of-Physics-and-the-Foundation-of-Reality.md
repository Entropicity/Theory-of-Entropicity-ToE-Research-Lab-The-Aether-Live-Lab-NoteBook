# Entropy as the Generator of Spacetime: Obidi's Foundational Thoughts and Case for Entropy as the Fundamental Field of Physics and the Foundation of Reality 

This paper integrates Obidi's insight — that entropy is the generator of spacetime because spacetime is nothing more than the ordered differentiation of informational points.

---

A central thesis of the Theory of Entropicity is that entropy is not merely a measure of disorder, uncertainty, or microstate multiplicity, but the primitive field from which spacetime itself emerges. This claim becomes unavoidable once one examines the informational structure underlying physical space.

In conventional physics, entropy is treated as a secondary quantity: a bookkeeping device for microstates, a measure of uncertainty, or a thermodynamic descriptor. Space and time, by contrast, are assumed to exist independently as geometric backgrounds upon which physical processes unfold. The Theory of Entropicity reverses this hierarchy. It asserts that spacetime cannot exist without entropy, because the very notion of spatial or temporal differentiation presupposes an underlying informational gradient.

To see this, consider the simplest requirement for physical space to be meaningful: two points must be distinguishable. If every point carried identical informational content, then no observer could identify separation, extension, or geometry. A universe in which all informational states are identical is a universe with no spatial structure, because there is no basis upon which to define “here” versus “there.” Spatial extension is therefore equivalent to an ordering of informational states, and this ordering is precisely what entropy quantifies.

Entropy, in its most fundamental interpretation, is a measure of how different one informational configuration is from another. When physical space appears as a continuum of points, it is because the underlying informational manifold contains a continuum of distinguishable states. The “distance” between two spatial points is nothing more than the degree of informational differentiation between them. Thus, space is the visible manifestation of entropy gradients.

The same reasoning applies to time. Temporal progression requires that the informational state of the universe at one moment differ from its state at another. If all informational states were identical across successive instants, time would collapse into a single undifferentiated moment. Temporal flow is therefore the ordered succession of informational changes, and entropy provides the measure of this ordering. Time is not an external parameter; it is the index of informational evolution.

Consequently, spacetime — the union of spatial differentiation and temporal succession — is not a primitive geometric arena. It is the emergent structure generated by entropy. The entropic manifold, equipped with the Obidi metric, provides the underlying geometry of distinguishability. Spacetime arises as a projection of this manifold, where entropy gradients define directions, separations, and causal structure.

This perspective aligns with and strengthens the entropic origin of Bogoliubov mixing discussed earlier. If particle creation, observer dependence, and mode mixing arise from entropic displacement rather than geometric slicing, then entropy must be recognized as the substrate beneath quantum fields and spacetime geometry alike. The entropic Bogoliubov coefficients do not merely reinterpret known phenomena; they reveal that the mechanisms traditionally attributed to spacetime curvature are in fact consequences of entropy-driven differentiation.

Thus, the Theory of Entropicity establishes a unified and unavoidable conclusion:  
Spacetime exists because entropy exists.  
Without entropy, there is no differentiation, no ordering, no geometry, no flow — and therefore no spacetime. Entropy is the generator, and spacetime is its emergent shadow.
