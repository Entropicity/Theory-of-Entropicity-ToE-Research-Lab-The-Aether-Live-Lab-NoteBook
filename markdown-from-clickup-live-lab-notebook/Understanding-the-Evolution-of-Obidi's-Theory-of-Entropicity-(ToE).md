# Understanding the Evolution of Obidi's Theory of Entropicity (ToE)

John Onimisi Obidi’s Theory of Entropicity (ToE) is a radical framework in theoretical physics that redefines entropy not as a passive measure of disorder, but as the fundamental dynamic field from which space, time, gravity, and quantum mechanics emerge.The evolution of the theory has moved from abstract statistical mathematics to a unifying physical framework:The Ontological Shift: Traditional physics treats entropy as an epistemic tool or a derivative of microstates. Obidi elevated entropy (S) to a universal, generative field (S(x)) that acts as the very substance of reality.From Information to Geometry: Building on information geometry, Obidi maps abstract statistical distinguishability (how "different" probability distributions are) to physical distances. The theory utilizes the Fisher-Rao metric to explain how information geometry naturally translates into physical spacetime curvature.The Obidi Action: Serving as the foundation of the theory, this principle (analogous to the Einstein-Hilbert action in General Relativity) describes how the universe optimizes and continuously redistributes entropy. It gives rise to the Master Entropic Equation (MEE), which governs how entropy gradients dictate physical forces.Reimagining Physical Constants: The theory reframes the speed of light (c) as the maximum rate at which the universal entropic field can redistribute information and change states.Ontodynamics and Philosophy: Expanding beyond physics, the theory establishes "Ontodynamics"—the study of existence itself as entropic motion.
