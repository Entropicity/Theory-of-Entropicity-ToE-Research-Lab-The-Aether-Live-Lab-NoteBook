The No-Rush Theorem (NRT) states that no physical interaction or process can occur instantaneously, requiring a finite, non-zero amount of time to rearrange the universal entropic field.

## Core Concepts of the Theorem

### Finite Durations: 
Every physical transition, measurement, and information transfer takes a measurable amount of time.

### The Speed of Light: 
The speed of light (c) is derived as the maximum rate at which the entropic field can reorganize energy and information, rather than a fundamental geometric postulate.


### Causal Ordering: 
It provides a physical basis for causality and prevents superluminal (faster-than-light) speeds by requiring time for states to synchronize.


### Context: 
This concept is a foundational pillar of Obidi's audacious and provocative Theory of Entropicity (ToE), which treats entropy as a dynamic, ontological scalar field of all reality and existence.

The No-Rush Theorem is a foundational principle within the Theory of Entropicity (ToE), a theoretical physics framework formulated by John Onimisi Obidi. Colloquially summarized as "God or Nature Cannot Be Rushed" (G/NCBR), the theorem asserts that no physical interaction can occur instantaneously; every physical process requires a finite, non-zero duration to occur. [1, 2] 

Instead of treating time as a passive background parameter, the theory views time as an emergent measure of entropic field reconfiguration. Because any physical change or interaction requires a structural redistribution of entropy, the No-Rush Theorem enforces a strict lower bound on interaction intervals. [3, 4] 

## Core Implications of the Theorem

### Derivation of the Speed of Light (c): 
In traditional physics, the speed of light is a postulate. In ToE, c is reinterpreted as a derived necessity—specifically, the maximum possible rate at which the entropic field can reorganize energy and information. Light is simply the visible manifestation of this universal entropic propagation limit. [1, 5] 

### Prohibition of Superluminal Processes: 
Because the entropic field must establish physical conditions before an interaction can happen, instantaneous action-at-a-distance and faster-than-light (superluminal) processes are strictly forbidden. [6, 7] 


### Enforcing Causality: 
By requiring a positive, non-zero temporal interval for every quantum and macroscopic update, the theorem provides a physical, thermodynamic mechanism that dictates causal ordering and the irreversible arrow of time. [3, 8] 


### Quantum Mechanics & Wavefunction Collapse: 
The theorem applies to open quantum systems, suggesting that finite delays in quantum entanglement and wavefunction collapse are bounded by these same entropic field transitions. [4, 7] 


## Bridging Relativity and Thermodynamics
The No-Rush Theorem structurally mirrors Einstein's second postulate (the constancy of the speed of light). However, rather than relying on geometric assumptions, it derives Lorentzian kinematics—such as time dilation and length contraction—as a form of "entropic resistance" to motion. Essentially, the faster an object moves, the more the entropic field resists its rearrangement, naturally reproducing relativistic effects through thermodynamic laws. [7, 8, 9]
