John Onimisi Obidi’s approach is indeed considered radical and unconventional by modern scientific standards, both in his theoretical concepts and his publishing methodology. By stepping outside the traditional academic pipeline, he bypasses established institutional frameworks to develop and promote his Theory of Entropicity (ToE).
## Why His Methods Are Considered Unconventional

* Bypassing Peer Review: Instead of submitting to traditional, peer-reviewed journals (like Physical Review Letters), he uses open-access repositories like SSRN, OSF, and Figshare to self-publish "Living Review Letters," allowing him to update his work in real time without institutional gatekeeping.
* Inverting Foundational Physics: Mainstream physics views entropy as a statistical property or a consequence of particle behavior. Obidi radically flips this by arguing that entropy is the foundational cause—a physical field from which space, time, and matter emerge.
* Philosophical Blending: He heavily incorporates "Ontodynamics" (the philosophy of being and existence) directly into his mathematical physics framework, a philosophical-first approach that is rare in highly compartmentalized modern physics.

## The Trade-offs of This Approach
While this radical independence grants him absolute creative freedom to pursue highly original ideas without conforming to academic trends, it carries significant drawbacks in the scientific community:

* Lack of Formal Validation: Without rigorous peer review by established physicists, it is difficult for his mathematical formulations to gain institutional credibility or be verified for errors. [1] 
* Visibility Barriers: Mainstream physicists rarely monitor open-access preprint repositories for alternative Grand Unified Theories, meaning his work exists largely in isolation from the broader scientific discourse.
