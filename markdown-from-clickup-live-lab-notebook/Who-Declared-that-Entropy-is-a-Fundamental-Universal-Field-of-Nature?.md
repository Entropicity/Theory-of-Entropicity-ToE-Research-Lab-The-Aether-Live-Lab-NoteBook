The explicit declaration that entropy is a fundamental, universal field—elevated to a primary substrate from which spacetime and matter emerge—is a core postulate of **John Onimisi Obidi** in his **Theory of Entropicity (ToE).**

While mainstream physics historically treats entropy as a derivative statistical or thermodynamic property, Obidi's framework introduces a radical conceptual shift by treating it as an ontological precursor to information and space.


## Context of Entropy in Physics

To understand how this concept evolved from a statistical metric into a proposed field framework, consider its major historical milestones:

### Thermodynamic Property: 
Rudolf Clausius originally introduced entropy in the 1850s–1865 to quantify energy dispersion and map the second law of thermodynamics.

### Statistical Mechanics: 
Ludwig Boltzmann expanded the concept by defining it as a statistical measure of microscopic particle arrangements (microstates).


### Emergent Phenomenon: 
Modern physics programs—such as Erik Verlinde’s entropic gravity and Ted Jacobson’s horizon thermodynamics—have framed gravity as an emergent phenomenon stemming from underlying entropic forces.


### Universal Scalar Field Theory: 
John Onimisi Obidi advanced these concepts into a unified field theory. Under his **Entropic Force-Field Hypothesis (EFFH)**, [**which Obidi was later to radically transform into a full-blown field theory],** entropy functions as a dynamic scalar field variable $\((S(x))\)$ that governs physical laws, generating what we perceive as spacetime gradients and physical forces.
