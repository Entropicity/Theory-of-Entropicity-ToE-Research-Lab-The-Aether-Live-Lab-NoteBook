# Scholium 


The difficulty inherent in Obidi's radical proposition about entropy could explain caution about making a universal claim, but it would be historically inaccurate to say that others avoided proposing emergent spacetime before Obidi. Several researchers explicitly explored related possibilities, but definitely not in the same radical stature as Obidi.

The distinction is between proposing that spacetime emerges from deeper information-related structure and proposing, specifically, that entropy itself is a fundamental physical field from which spacetime and all physical laws emerge.

## Some relevant precedents are:

Researcher	| Earlier proposal	| Important distinction

## Ted Jacobson, 1995	
Derived Einstein’s equation from thermodynamic assumptions involving entropy and local horizons.	The argument uses local spacetime structure; it does not construct spacetime entirely from a pregeometric entropy field, which is where Obidi offers his radical departure.


## Ariel Caticha, 2005	
Investigated spatial geometry as a manifestation of underlying statistical structure, using entropy, information geometry, and entropic dynamics.	This is a close conceptual predecessor, although its inferential interpretation differs from Obidi’s proposed physical entropy field.


## Mark Van Raamsdonk, 2010	
Argued that the emergence of connected spacetime is closely related to quantum entanglement.	The underlying structure is quantum and entanglement-based, rather than Obidi’s universal entropic field.


These are documented research proposals, not merely retrospective similarities. 

The point about the size of the problem nevertheless stands. A universal entropy-first proposal, such as Obidi's, takes on several demanding obligations simultaneously: 

* defining entropy without presupposing spacetime, 

* recovering spatial and temporal structure, 

* explaining causal relations, and 

* reproducing established dynamics.

That difficulty gives a methodological reason to make narrower claims. A researcher can demonstrate a relationship between entropy and gravitational equations without claiming to have established the ultimate nature of reality. We cannot infer that this restraint reflects fear, failure to notice the possibility, or a deliberate rejection of Obidi’s exact proposition.

For ToE, the useful question of originality is therefore: what specific physical assumptions, mathematical constructions, or results does Obidi add to those earlier approaches? 

His identification of entropy as the fundamental physical field is a clear statement of his intended foundation. That that particular formulation is historically unique does not for now require any more comprehensive comparison.

Even the existence of predecessors does not make Obidi's investigation pointless. It gives Obidi concrete work to build on—and makes it possible to explain precisely where Obidi agrees with earlier investigators, where he departs from them, and what that departure enables him to derive.

# Scholium 



The specific works cited above do not make the same combined, sweeping proposition as Obidi’s three conjectures. They establish related precedents, but they do not establish equivalence with Obidi's position. The response in the earlier scholium gave too much weight to that comparison and insufficient attention to the scope Obidi is emphasizing in the Theory of Entropicity (ToE).

## Obidi’s proposition combines three commitments:

# 1. Entropy is the fundamental physical field of reality.

# 2. All physical laws and interactions are derivable from that field.

# 3. Physical spacetime itself emerges from an entropic–informational manifold.


Taken together, these propose a comprehensive foundation for physics. Showing that someone previously connected entropy with gravity, or entanglement with spacetime, does not demonstrate that they proposed that same extensive and comprehensive foundation for physics and science.

We can therefore now state our position more precisely than we did in all of the foregoing: 

> # **Perhaps the difficulty of supporting such a comprehensive entropy-first account helps explain why some researchers make narrower, more restricted claims. That is a reasonable possibility. What we cannot establish without evidence is whether those researchers considered Obidi’s exact proposition and deliberately refrained from making it.**

The distinction we are defending is valid: 
> # **Related intellectual foundations do not erase a difference in the scope and physical meaning of a proposal. Recognizing that difference does not require us to settle its historical uniqueness or physical correctness first.**
