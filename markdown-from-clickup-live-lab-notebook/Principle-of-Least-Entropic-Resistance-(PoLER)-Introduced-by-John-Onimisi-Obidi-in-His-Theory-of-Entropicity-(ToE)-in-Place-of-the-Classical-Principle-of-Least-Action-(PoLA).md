## Conceptual Foundations: Mechanics vs. Thermodynamics
The transition from the Classical Principle of Least Action (PoLA) to the Principle of Least Entropic Resistance represents a shift from a deterministic, geometry-first universe to an informational, entropy-first universe.

### The Principle of Least Action (PoLA): 
Historically championed by **Maupertuis, Euler, Lagrange,** and **Hamilton**, the PoLA states that a physical system will always follow a path that minimizes (or makes stationary) a quantity called action ($S$). Action is typically defined as the integral over time of the Lagrangian ($L = T - V$, the difference between kinetic and potential energy). It assumes space, time, and forces are primary entities.

### The Principle of Least Entropic Resistance (PoLER): 
Introduced by John Onimisi Obidi within his Theory of Entropicity (ToE), PoLER asserts that nature does not care about balancing kinetic and potential mechanical energies. Instead, a system evolves along a trajectory that minimizes the net information-theoretic or thermodynamic resistance offered by the surrounding universe. 

In this view [in Obidi's Theory of Entropicity (ToE)], space, time, and forces are not primary; they are emergent phenomena arising from the gradients of an underlying universal entropy field.

By switching from PLA to PoLER, our understanding of foundational physics concepts changes completely:

Classical physics treats inertia as an inherent property of mass. PoLER defines inertia as the measure of a system's local thermodynamic resistance to changing its current informational state.
