# Philosophical Boldness that Historically Drives Scientific Revolutions: Obidi's Entropia and Entropions in the Theory of Entropicity (ToE)

"Obidi's Entropia" relates to the Theory of Entropicity (ToE), a radical and audacious alternative physics framework proposed primarily by independent author and researcher John Onimisi Obidi starting around early 2025. [1] 

Rather than viewing entropy as merely a passive measure of thermodynamic disorder or decay, Obidi's theory conceptualizes it as a fundamental, dynamic ontic field—denoted as S(x)—from which all physical concepts like space, time, matter, and gravity emerge. [1, 2] 

## Core Principles of the Theory of Entropicity (ToE)

### The Entropic Field: 
The theory flips the traditional paradigm of physics on its head. Spacetime does not contain entropy; rather, a continuous entropic manifold generates physical spacetime and matter. Each point in the universe holds a local informational state of "distinguishability". [2, 3] 

### The Obidi Action Principle (OAP): 
Serving as a variational framework analogous to the Principle of Least Action in classical mechanics, this principle dictates how the entropic field evolves dynamically over time. [4, 5] 

### The Master Entropic Equation (MEE): 
Also referred to as the Obidi Field Equations (OFE), this core mathematical equation plays a role similar to Einstein's field equations in General Relativity. It models gravity not as a geometric curvature of spacetime, but as an emergent "entropic pressure" caused by gradients in the underlying field. [1, 2, 6] 

### The No-Rush Theorem (NRT): 
This theorem argues that time itself is an emergent property born out of the finite rate at which the universal entropic field can synchronize informational states. Under this view, the speed of light (c) represents the cosmic speed limit for how fast reality can reconfigure its entropic states. [2, 7] 

### Entropions: 
The framework posits hypothetical quantized excitations of this fundamental field, which are referred to as "entropions". [8] 

## Academic and Practical Standing
The Theory of Entropicity (ToE) is a highly audacious and provocative physics framework visible on various platforms, academic repositories and distribution hubs. 

While its literature attempts to mathematically map itself to established concepts—like using the Fisher–Rao metric or explaining Mercury's perihelion precession—it currently remains outside standard mainstream physics and is undergoing formal peer review, scrutiny and empirical validation by the broader scientific community. # Obidi's Philosophical Boldness

Obidi's Theory of Entropicity (ToE) is absolutely an audacious and highly ambitious attempt to rethink the foundational architecture of the universe. 

In modern theoretical physics, many of the greatest roadblocks—like the incompatibility between General Relativity and Quantum Mechanics—stem from how we view spacetime. 

Obidi's approach of treating spacetime as an emergent phenomenon rather than a fixed stage aligns philosophically with some mainstream concepts, even if his specific formulation remains highly provocative and disruptive, as well as uniquely imaginative at once.

By elevating entropy from a statistical side effect of thermodynamics into the primary, creative force of reality, Obidi's theoretical physics framework mirrors and builds upon several profound ideas in theoretical physics, information geometry, and information theory:

### Thermodynamic Gravity: 
Physicists like Tanu Padmanabhan and Erik Verlinde have similarly argued that gravity is not a fundamental force, but an emergent "entropic force" arising from the statistical behavior of microscopic degrees of freedom.

### The Holographic Principle: 
Popularized by Gerard 't Hooft and Leonard Susskind, this principle suggests our 3D universe is a projection of information stored on a distant 2D boundary, heavily tying the nature of space to information theory.

### Quantum Information Theory: 
Modern "It from bit" philosophies suggest that physical matter and fields are ultimately derived from underlying information states.

What makes Obidi's Theory of Entropicity (ToE) particularly bold is the attempt to codify this into a strict, unified mathematical structure—complete with the **Obidi Action Principle** and **Entropions**—**to challenge Einstein's geometric worldview directly.** 

Whether such a theory stands up to rigorous peer review and empirical testing, there is no longer any doubt that Obidi's willingness to radically re-imagine the nature of reality is exactly the kind of philosophical boldness that historically drives scientific revolutions in all ages.
