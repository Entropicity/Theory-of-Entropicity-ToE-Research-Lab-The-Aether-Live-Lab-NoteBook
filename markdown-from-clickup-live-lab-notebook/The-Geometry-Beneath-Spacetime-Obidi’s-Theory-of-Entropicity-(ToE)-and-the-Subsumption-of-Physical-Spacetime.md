# The Geometry Beneath Spacetime: Obidi’s Theory of Entropicity (ToE) and the Subsumption of Physical Spacetime within the Entropic-Informational Manifold—Comparison with Other Researchers: Jacobson, Verlinde, Bianconi, Van Raamsdonk, Ryu and Takayanagi

## Abstract
This paper presents a technical exposition of the current distinctive position of Obidi’s Theory of Entropicity (ToE): physical spacetime geometry is not fundamentally separate from information geometry, but is a physically actualized Lorentzian subgeometry of a broader Entropic-Informational Manifold. The theory departs from approaches that derive gravitational field equations from thermodynamics, interpret gravity as an entropic force, reconstruct spacetime from holographic entanglement, or formulate gravity through entropy-based actions. 
ToE instead proposes a subsumption thesis: the physical spacetime manifold is a constrained event-sector within a higher-dimensional entropic-information geometry. In this framework, spacetime coordinates are compressed event-data coordinates; the spacetime metric is induced from a deeper entropic-information metric; physical intervals are restricted forms of a broader informational interval; and gravity is curvature of the actualized spacetime sector. 
The paper compares this formulation with major neighboring programs and identifies the specific originality of ToE as the claim that spacetime is not merely emergent from information, but is contained within entropic-information geometry as its physically registered submanifold.

# 1. Introduction
The central problem of modern foundational physics is not simply the unification of general relativity and quantum mechanics. Beneath that formal problem lies a deeper ontological question: what is the status of spacetime itself? Is spacetime fundamental, or is it the macroscopic appearance of a deeper informational, thermodynamic, quantum, or entropic structure?

Several research programs have already challenged the primacy of spacetime. 
Jacobson famously derived Einstein’s equation from horizon thermodynamics, treating the Einstein equation as an equation of state derived from entropy-area proportionality and the Clausius relation δQ = TdS. 

Verlinde proposed that gravity may be understood as an entropic force associated with changes in information and emergent space in a holographic setting. 
Ryu and Takayanagi connected entanglement entropy in conformal field theory to geometric areas in holographic duality, while Van Raamsdonk argued that classical spacetime connectedness is deeply related to quantum entanglement. 

Bianconi’s recent “Gravity from entropy” proposes an entropic action based on quantum relative entropy between spacetime geometry and matter-induced geometry.

Obidi’s Theory of Entropicity (ToE) enters this landscape with a different claim. It does not merely say that gravity has thermodynamic aspects, nor merely that gravity is an entropic force, nor merely that entanglement helps build geometry. Its current formulation is stronger:

Physical spacetime geometry is the Lorentzian event-sector of a higher-dimensional Entropic-Informational Manifold.
In symbolic form:
𝔖⁴ ⊂ 𝔈ᴺ

Here, 𝔈ᴺ denotes the total Entropic-Informational Manifold, and 𝔖⁴ denotes the physical spacetime manifold. The assertion is not that spacetime geometry resembles information geometry, but that physical spacetime geometry is contained within, induced from, and actualized out of entropic-information geometry.

This paper develops that thesis in technical form and explains how it distinguishes ToE from neighboring approaches.
