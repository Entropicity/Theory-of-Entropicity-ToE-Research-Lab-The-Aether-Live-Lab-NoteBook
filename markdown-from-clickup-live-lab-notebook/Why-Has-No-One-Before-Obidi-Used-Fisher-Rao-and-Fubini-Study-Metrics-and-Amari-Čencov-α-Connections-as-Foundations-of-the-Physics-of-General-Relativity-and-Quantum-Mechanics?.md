# But the similarities between information Geometry and physical spacetime are two unmistakable to be taken lightly or just thrust away without serious consideration

Those structural parallels are indeed too striking to dismiss as mere mathematical coincidence. In modern theoretical physics, recognizing deep structural isomorphisms—like the link between thermodynamics and black hole mechanics in the 1970s—has repeatedly preceded major breakthroughs.

Key Parallels Driving Serious Investigation

• Invariant Metric Structures: Just as spacetime intervals in General Relativity remain invariant under diffeomorphisms, the Fisher–Rao metric is the unique Riemannian metric invariant under sufficient statistics (Čencov’s theorem), and the Fubini–Study metric is uniquely invariant under unitary quantum transformations. Both domains naturally select a single, rigid geometric distance based on distinguishability.
• Curvature as Interaction and Uncertainty: In General Relativity, Riemannian curvature manifests physically as gravity. In information geometry, curvature on statistical and quantum manifolds quantifies irreducible statistical uncertainty (the Cramér–Rao bound) and quantum non-commutativity, behaving mathematically like a physical field strength.
• Thermodynamics as Geometry: Long before recent pre-geometric proposals, physicists like George Ruppeiner and Frank Weinhold showed that thermodynamic state spaces carry Riemannian metrics whose curvature diverges at phase transitions—mirroring spacetime singularities.
• Emergent Gravity Precedents: Work by Ted Jacobson (deriving Einstein's equations from thermodynamic state equations) and Erik Verlinde (entropic gravity) demonstrated that gravitational dynamics can emerge directly from informational and entropic principles, lending credibility to deeper geometric formulations.

Why Mainstream Physics Remains Cautious

While physicists take these mathematical links seriously—especially in quantum information, AdS/CFT, and holographic entanglement entropy—they generally require a framework to pass two strict tests before accepting an ontological shift:
• Recovering Known Limits: Any pre-geometric theory must cleanly reproduce standard Lorentzian signature (\(-, +, +, +\)), gauge symmetries, and the exact Einstein-Hilbert and Standard Model actions without fine-tuning.
• New Empirical Signatures: A deeper geometric foundation must predict a measurable phenomenon—such as a specific correction to black hole ringdowns, cosmological inflation, or quantum decoherence—that standard theory cannot explain.
Bridging that gap between undeniable mathematical elegance and concrete, testable physical predictions is precisely where the frontier of theoretical physics sits today.
