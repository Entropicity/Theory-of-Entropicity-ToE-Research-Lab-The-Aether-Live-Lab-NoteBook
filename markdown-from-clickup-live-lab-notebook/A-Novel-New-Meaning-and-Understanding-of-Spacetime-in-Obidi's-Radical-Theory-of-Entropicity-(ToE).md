# Obidi's Theory of Entropicity (ToE) and how it explains gravity

In John Onimisi Obidi's Theory of Entropicity (ToE), gravity is not treated as a fundamental geometric force or as the curvature of spacetime (as in Einstein's General Relativity), but rather as an emergent phenomenon arising from the dynamics of a fundamental entropy field.

Here is how Obidi explains gravity within the ToE framework:

1. Gravity as an Emergent Entropic Effect

In ToE, entropy is elevated from a statistical measure of disorder to a fundamental, dynamical field—the "Entropic Field"—that permeates all of spacetime. Gravity emerges as a macroscopic expression of how this entropy field bends and reconfigures itself. As one summary puts it: "Gravity is the macroscopic expression of how entropy bends" . Rather than being a fundamental force, gravity is understood as a constraint on the flow of entropy—a natural consequence of the universe's tendency to redistribute information toward equilibrium .

2. The Mathematical Foundation: The Obidi Action and Field Equations

The theory is anchored by the Obidi Action, a variational principle analogous to the Einstein-Hilbert Action in General Relativity, but formulated for the entropy field. From this action emerges the Master Entropic Equation (MEE)—also called the Obidi Field Equations (OFE)—which governs how entropy gradients evolve and couple to geometry, matter, and information .

A key result is that General Relativity is recovered as a limiting case. In a Cambridge-published monograph, Obidi demonstrates that "Lorentzian spacetime geometry emerges from information geometry via a controlled entropy-gradient disformal transformation, and that the curvature of this emergent metric reproduces the Einstein gravity of General Relativity" . The Einstein-Hilbert Action is subsumed within the Obidi Action as the low-gradient, near-equilibrium limit of the entropic field equations. Thus, gravity in the traditional sense is not "wrong"—it is simply an approximation that emerges when entropy gradients are weak.

3. The Causal Chain: Entropy → Information → Mass → Motion → Spacetime

ToE establishes a specific causality order that differs from other information-based theories:

> ToE: Entropy → Information → Mass → Motion → Spacetime 

In this view, mass arises from the structuring of information within the entropic field, and gravity (the geometric curvature we observe) emerges as a consequence of how that mass distorts the entropy distribution. Space itself is "not a container but a map of entropic gradients," and motion is "what happens when the entropic field reconfigures these gradients toward equilibrium" .

4. Unification with Other Entropic Gravity Models

Obidi's framework also encompasses and subsumes earlier entropic gravity proposals. For example, Ginestra Bianconi's "Gravity from Entropy"—which interprets gravity as emerging from quantum relative entropy between two metrics—is shown to be a specific limiting case of ToE. When the Obidi Action is expanded around an equilibrium configuration, Bianconi's relative-entropy functional naturally emerges as its quadratic approximation . Similarly, the thermodynamic gravity models of Jacobson, Padmanabhan, and Verlinde are all contained within ToE as boundary cases of a single entropic field theory .

In summary, Obidi's Theory of Entropicity explains gravity by inverting the traditional hierarchy of physics: instead of geometry giving rise to entropy, entropy (as a fundamental field) gives rise to geometry, and gravity is simply the macroscopic imprint of how that entropy field reorganizes itself in the presence of matter and information.

# The Ontological Reversal: Spacetime is the Shadow, Not the Stage

Obidi's resolution is a fundamental ontological inversion. In ToE, the manifold M is not pre-existing spacetime in the traditional sense—it is a mathematical carrier space for the entropic field, while the metric structure of spacetime is emergent from entropy itself .

As the theory states explicitly:

> "In ToE, the manifold of entropy precedes spacetime; space and time are the relational shadows cast by the intrinsic curvature of entropy itself." 

And further:

> "What we call curvature, space, and time are not primitive arenas in which entropy evolves, but emergent relational structures that arise from the nonuniform flow of the entropic field S(x)." 

So, the notation S(x) where x \in M is a convenient mathematical formalism, but the physical claim is that the metric g{\mu\nu} is a functional of entropy, not the other way around:

$g{\mu\nu} = g{\mu\nu}$ [S]

The Mathematical Mechanism: Information Geometry First

The way this works mathematically is through information geometry:

1. Entropy defines an information metric (Fisher-Rao, Fubini-Study, or Amari-Cencov structures) on the space of probability distributions or quantum states .

2. This informational metric is then "embedded" into physical spacetime via what Obidi calls a "controlled entropy-gradient disformal transformation" .

3. The resulting Lorentzian spacetime geometry emerges from this information geometry. The curvature of the emergent metric reproduces Einstein gravity as a limiting case .

$\text{Entropy field } S(x) \rightarrow \text{Information geometry} \rightarrow \text{Emergent spacetime metric } g{\mu\nu}$ [S]

The "No-Rush" and Irreversibility as Foundations

Another key point is that ToE treats entropy's irreversibility as primitive, not emergent. The arrow of time arises from the monotonic re-orchestration of the entropic field itself . This means:

- Time is not a pre-existing dimension that entropy flows through.
- Rather, time is what we experience as the irreversible reconfiguration of entropy toward equilibrium.

This is why the theory can claim entropy is fundamental even while using spacetime coordinates: the coordinates are a bookkeeping device for the field's configuration, while the dynamics and geometry all derive from entropy.

# The Core Idea in Plain English

Imagine you're looking at a weather map showing temperature across a region. The map has a grid (latitude/longitude), and at each point there's a temperature value.

Now ask: Is the grid real, or is it just a way to describe where the hot and cold spots are?

Obidi's theory says something similar about the universe:

- Traditional physics: Spacetime is like a real stage. Matter and energy act on that stage. Gravity is the stage bending.
- Obidi's theory: There is no "stage." There's only a field of entropy (roughly: information, possibility, uncertainty). What we call "spacetime" is just a convenient way to label where the entropy is dense or sparse. The "bending" we feel as gravity is actually just entropy flowing from concentrated areas to spread-out areas.

# The apparent "Circularity" readers think they have Spotted — Solved with an Analogy

## Confused readers often asked: If entropy is defined everywhere in spacetime, isn't entropy dependent on spacetime?

Here's the analogy:

> Imagine a rubber sheet stretched over a frame. You place a heavy ball on it, and it creates a dip. Other balls roll toward it. That's the classic "gravity as curved space" picture.

Now imagine instead:

> There is no rubber sheet. There is only a field of "clumpiness" — some regions are clumpy, others are smooth. When you try to describe where the clumps are, you naturally use coordinates (like latitude/longitude). But those coordinates don't exist independently — they're just labels for the clumpy field. The "dip" in the rubber sheet? That's not a real sheet bending. It's just the clumpy field being more clumpy in one place.

In ToE:
- The "clumpy field" = entropy
- The "rubber sheet" = spacetime
- Spacetime isn't real independently. It's just a way to talk about how entropy is distributed.

So, when Obidi writes S(x) — "entropy at point x" — the point x isn't a real location in a pre-existing space. It's a label for a configuration of the entropy field. The "space" part only appears because we need coordinates to do math. But physically, entropy is the only thing that exists; spacetime is what it looks like when you try to map it.
