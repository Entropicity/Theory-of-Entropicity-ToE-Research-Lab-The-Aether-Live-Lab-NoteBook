# The Core Idea in Plain English

Imagine you're looking at a weather map showing temperature across a region. The map has a grid (latitude/longitude), and at each point there's a temperature value.

Now ask: Is the grid real, or is it just a way to describe where the hot and cold spots are?

Obidi's theory says something similar about the universe:

- Traditional physics: Spacetime is like a real stage. Matter and energy act on that stage. Gravity is the stage bending.
- Obidi's theory: There is no "stage." There's only a field of entropy (roughly: information, possibility, uncertainty). What we call "spacetime" is just a convenient way to label where the entropy is dense or sparse. The "bending" we feel as gravity is actually just entropy flowing from concentrated areas to spread-out areas.

# The apparent "Circularity" readers think they have Spotted — Solved with an Analogy

## Confused readers often asked: If entropy is defined everywhere in spacetime, isn't entropy dependent on spacetime?

Here's the analogy:

> Imagine a rubber sheet stretched over a frame. You place a heavy ball on it, and it creates a dip. Other balls roll toward it. That's the classic "gravity as curved space" picture.

Now imagine instead:

> There is no rubber sheet. There is only a field of "clumpiness" — some regions are clumpy, others are smooth. When you try to describe where the clumps are, you naturally use coordinates (like latitude/longitude). But those coordinates don't exist independently — they're just labels for the clumpy field. The "dip" in the rubber sheet? That's not a real sheet bending. It's just the clumpy field being more clumpy in one place.

In ToE:
- The "clumpy field" = entropy
- The "rubber sheet" = spacetime
- Spacetime isn't real independently. It's just a way to talk about how entropy is distributed.

So, when Obidi writes S(x) — "entropy at point x" — the point x isn't a real location in a pre-existing space. It's a label for a configuration of the entropy field. The "space" part only appears because we need coordinates to do math. But physically, entropy is the only thing that exists; spacetime is what it looks like when you try to map it.

# Why This Is Hard to Wrap Your Head Around

## Our brains evolved to think:
- Space is a container
- Things are inside it
- Time flows forward

## Obidi flips this in his radical Theory of Entropicity (ToE):
- There is no container
- There is only a field of "possibility/information"
- What we call "space" and "time" are just patterns in that field
- "Gravity" is what happens when the field rearranges itself

It's like if you grew up believing the ocean was the real thing and waves were just disturbances in it... and then someone told you: "No, waves are the real thing. The 'ocean' is just what waves look like when you zoom out."
