# From Shannon Entropy to Gravity in the Theory of Entropicity (ToE)

# Prologue 

Obidi's Theory of Entropicity (ToE) does not discard the work of earlier researchers. Rather, it attempts to synthesize and extend several existing lines of research—**information theory, information geometry, thermodynamics,** and **emergent gravity**—by advancing a stronger ontological hypothesis: 

> that entropy itself is the universal foundational field from which physical laws, spacetime, and gravity emerge.

That characterization recognizes both the intellectual continuity with prior work and the distinctive ambition of Obidi's radical proposal.

## Introduction 
The conceptual originality of the Theory of Entropicity (ToE) is perhaps most evident in Obidi's methodological decision to begin with Shannon entropy as the primary physical quantity from which gravitational phenomena emerge. Whereas conventional approaches generally regard Shannon entropy as a statistical measure of uncertainty or information, ToE proposes that entropy possesses an ontological status as a locally distributed physical field whose spatial and temporal variations influence the dynamics of physical systems.

# Distinguishability from a Scalar Field 
Within this framework, the distinguishability of microscopic configurations is represented by a continuous scalar field,

$\Phi(x)$,

which characterizes the local entropic state of the physical system. Rather than treating entropy merely as a bookkeeping device for counting microstates, ToE postulates that gradients, transport, and curvature of this entropy field generate the structures ordinarily attributed to gravitation.

## Comparison with Verlinde's Entropic Gravity 
This proposal differs substantially from earlier information-theoretic approaches to gravity. In particular, Verlinde's entropic gravity derives gravitational attraction as an emergent entropic force associated with holographic information stored on boundaries. While remarkably successful in reproducing Newtonian gravity and providing intriguing connections with holography and thermodynamics, Verlinde's formulation does not derive the full Einstein field equations from Shannon entropy itself, nor does it identify entropy as a local dynamical field defined throughout spacetime.

## From Boundary Entropy to Bulk Entropy 
Obidi's approach therefore shifts the mathematical starting point from boundary entropy to bulk entropy. In ToE, entropy is not simply a scalar state function assigned to horizons or equilibrium configurations but is promoted to a continuous field whose local dynamics are assumed to underlie spacetime curvature. The central hypothesis is that the geometry experienced as gravitation is an emergent manifestation of the geometry of the entropy field itself.

## Radical Proposal of Obidi's ToE 
The distinguishing feature of ToE is therefore not merely the use of entropy in gravitational theory—an idea that has appeared in various forms since the work of **Bekenstein, Hawking, Jacobson, Padmanabhan**, and **Verlinde**—but the specific proposal that Shannon entropy constitutes the fundamental dynamical substrate from which gravitational geometry emerges. Whether this proposal ultimately reproduces Einstein gravity in a mathematically rigorous and experimentally consistent manner remains a question for further theoretical development and empirical investigation, but it represents a conceptually distinct research programme within the broader effort to understand gravity as an emergent phenomenon.

## Entropy as the Foundation of Reality 
ToE subsequently extends this hypothesis by introducing additional mathematical structures that seek to elevate the entropy field from a scalar quantity to a source capable of generating relativistic spacetime dynamics. The success of this programme ultimately depends upon demonstrating that these structures reproduce the Einstein field equations, satisfy the principles of covariance and conservation, recover established gravitational phenomena in the appropriate limits, and make experimentally distinguishable predictions beyond General Relativity.

# Epilogue 
Regardless of whether Obidi's proposal ultimately proves correct, it is objectively an unquestionably bold conceptual move.

## Historically:
* **Claude Shannon** introduced entropy as a measure of information or uncertainty in communication theory.
* **Jacob Bekenstein** connected entropy with black holes.
* **Stephen Hawking** demonstrated that black holes possess thermodynamic properties.
* **Ted Jacobson** showed that Einstein's equations can be interpreted as an equation of state derived from thermodynamic considerations.
* **Erik Verlinde** proposed gravity as an emergent entropic force.

All of these works employ entropy in important ways. However, they generally do not begin by postulating that Shannon entropy itself is a local physical field whose dynamics generate spacetime geometry.

**That is the conceptual leap that ToE proposes.**

It is a bold move because it reverses the usual direction of reasoning. Instead of saying
spacetime geometry determines entropy,
or
gravity has a thermodynamic interpretation,
ToE proposes, in essence,
that entropy is the more fundamental entity, and spacetime geometry emerges from its dynamics.

**That is a significant inversion of the standard paradigm.**

## The boldness lies in several aspects:
### Promoting Shannon entropy from a statistical quantity to a physical field.
In conventional statistical mechanics and information theory, Shannon entropy measures uncertainty over probability distributions. Obidi's proposal assigns it a deeper ontological role as a locally defined field capable of participating in physical dynamics. That is a significant departure from standard treatments.

### Making entropy fundamental rather than derivative.
Much of modern theoretical physics treats entropy as something computed from an already existing physical system—black holes have entropy, quantum fields have entanglement entropy, thermodynamic systems have entropy. ToE reverses this logical order by proposing that entropy is the more fundamental entity from which spacetime geometry and gravity emerge.

### Attempting to derive Einsteinian gravity from information.
Many researchers have explored connections between information and gravity, including **Jacobson, Verlinde, Padmanabhan, and others**. However, these approaches generally begin from thermodynamics, horizon entropy, holography, or entanglement. ToE instead begins from Shannon entropy itself as the primitive object. That is a distinct conceptual starting point.

### Replacing geometry-first thinking with entropy-first thinking.
General Relativity (GR) starts with spacetime geometry. ToE proposes that geometry is secondary—that what we interpret as curvature is an emergent manifestation of a more fundamental entropic structure.
This is an ambitious inversion of the usual hierarchy.

However, boldness should not be conflated with correctness. The history of theoretical physics contains many bold ideas that were ultimately unsuccessful, alongside bold ideas that transformed science. The decisive question is whether the mathematical framework can:

* derive the Einstein field equations or a demonstrably superior theory,
* remain internally consistent,
* recover all established experimental tests of gravity,
* and produce novel, falsifiable predictions.

If those conditions are met, the proposal would represent a substantial advance. If they are not, it would remain an interesting but ultimately unsuccessful hypothesis.

So it is entirely fair to say:
Obidi's decision to begin with Shannon entropy as the fundamental physical field from which gravity emerges is an exceptionally bold conceptual hypothesis. Its originality lies not merely in connecting entropy with gravity, but in reversing the conventional hierarchy by proposing that entropy is the ontological substrate from which spacetime geometry itself emerges. 

Whether this bold proposal succeeds depends not on its originality but on the strength of its mathematical development and its agreement with experimental observation.
That characterization is both scientifically accurate and appropriately measured.
