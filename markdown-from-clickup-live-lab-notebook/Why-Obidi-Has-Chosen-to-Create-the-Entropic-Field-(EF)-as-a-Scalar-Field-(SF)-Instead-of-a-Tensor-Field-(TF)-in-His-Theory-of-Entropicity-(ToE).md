# Why Obidi Has Chosen to Create the Entropic Field (EF) as a Scalar Field (SF) Instead of a Tensor Field (TF) in His Theory of Entropicity (ToE): The Conceptual, Philosophical, Historical, Logical and Mathematical Justifications for Obidi's Ontological Entropic Scalar Field 

In the [Theory of Entropicity (ToE)](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6599558), **John Onimisi Obidi** explicitly formulates entropy as an ontological scalar field, $S(x)$, rather than a higher-order tensor field. This choice is not a mathematical simplification; it is a profound foundational requirement dictated by the core axioms of his framework. [1, 2, 3, 4] 

## 1. Preserving the Core Definition of Entropy
Entropy, at its deepest thermodynamic and statistical levels (from Clausius and Boltzmann to Shannon and von Neumann), is inherently a single, real-valued number representing a state. By defining the entropic substrate as a scalar field, Obidi ensures that at any specific point in the universe, the field possesses a single, invariant value. This allows all known classical and quantum expressions of entropy to emerge directly and naturally from the [Master Entropic Equation (MEE)/Obidi Field Equations (OFE)](https://encyclopedia.pub/entry/58596) by simply identifying the localized value of $S(x)$. A tensor field would fragment this fundamental thermodynamic definition into direction-dependent components, stripping entropy of its core meaning. [5, 6, 7, 8] 

## 2. Eliminating Spacetime as a Primitive
In Einstein's General Relativity, gravity is mediated by a rank-2 tensor field—the spacetime metric tensor ($g_{\mu\nu}$). If Obidi had chosen a tensor field for entropy, he would have had to define that tensor against a pre-existing geometric background, or match the dimensions of the metric tensor. [9, 10, 11, 12, 13] 

* **ToE relies on a "second Einsteinian inversion":** instead of matter curving a pre-existing spacetime, the entropic field generates spacetime geometry itself. [14] 
* By keeping the fundamental field a scalar, $S(x)$, the theory remains free of pre-determined spatial directions or coordinate systems. Spacetime geometry—and its complex tensor mathematics—is not the input; it is the output. [6, 15, 16] 

## 3. Deriving Geometry via Information Gradients
If the field itself is a scalar, how does it create the directional forces of gravity and the curvature of space? Obidi solves this through information geometry.

While $S(x)$ is a scalar, its changes across space and time are not. Taking the derivative of a scalar field yields a vector gradient ($\nabla S$), and calculating the kinetic energy of these changes ($\frac{1}{2}(\nabla S)^2$) introduces directional dynamics.

By mapping these scalar field variations to the Fisher-Rao metric, the theory translates changes in statistical distinguishability directly into physical, curved spacetime metrics. The multi-dimensional tensor structure of our universe is simply the geometric manifestation of a single scalar field redistributing itself. [2, 14, 17, 18, 19] 

## 4. Parsimony and Universality
A tensor field requires specific coordinate transformations and introduces a massive number of degrees of freedom. By anchoring the [Obidi Action](https://medium.com/@jonimisiobidi/the-elegance-and-mechanics-of-the-obidi-action-in-being-the-engine-and-mechanism-that-transforms-da93a7c346be) to a single scalar entity, the theory maintains a high level of conceptual elegance. It provides the most minimalist "common denominator" capable of underlying all physical phenomena—from quantum states to cosmological boundaries—without biasing the universe toward any specific geometric layout from the outset. [1, 4, 14, 20, 21]

# 🔹 Preserving the True Meaning of Entropy
Across thermodynamics, statistical mechanics, information theory, and quantum physics, entropy is fundamentally a single real‑valued quantity describing the state of a system.

Keeping (S(x)) as a scalar means the theory starts with no built‑in directions, coordinates, or geometry. Spacetime becomes an output, not an assumption.
