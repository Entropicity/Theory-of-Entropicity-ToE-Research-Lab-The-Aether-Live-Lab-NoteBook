# Ryu-Takayanagi Formula of Holographic Spacetime and Spacetime in Obidi's Theory of Entropicity (ToE) 

The previous description of smooth space emerging from discrete quantum bits relies on standard holographic frameworks (like AdS/CFT and the Ryu-Takayanagi formula). The description of spacetime in John Onimisi Obidi’s Theory of Entropicity (ToE) differs fundamentally by replacing the static geometry of quantum information with a dynamic, fluid, and continuous entropic field. [1, 2, 3, 4, 5] 

While both theories conclude that spacetime is an "emergent" illusion, they differ fundamentally in their foundational concepts: [3, 6] 

## 1. Structure vs. Flow (The Core Ontological Shift)

* Standard Holography: Spacetime is built from the static architecture of information. Discrete qubits weave space through geometric bonds of quantum entanglement. [1, 4] 
* Obidi’s ToE: Spacetime is animated by the dynamics of entropy flux. It eliminates quantum states and classical fields as fundamental pieces. Instead, it establishes a continuous, local "entropic field" ($S(x,t)$) as the primary substance of existence. In Obidi's framework, space maps entropic gradients, while time is the heartbeat of this field reconfiguring itself. [1, 3, 4, 5] 

## 2. The Mechanism of Curvature

* Standard Holography: Curvature is determined by the amount of entanglement across boundary surfaces, mathematically calculated via the Ryu-Takayanagi formula.
* Obidi’s ToE: Curvature is a derived product of colliding informational updates. Using information geometry primitives (like the Amari–Čencov connections), ToE states that without entropic gradients, there is no informational divergence, no Čencov tensor, and therefore no curvature or gravity. This leads to Obidi’s famous core phrase: "Entropy curves existence itself." [5, 7, 8] 

## 3. Continuous Field vs. Discrete Bits

* Standard Holography: Relies on a discrete boundary of quantum pixels or qubits that are structurally organized (like a digital tensor network or error-correcting code).
* Obidi’s ToE: Rejects the idea that discrete quantum mechanics is the absolute starting point. Instead, ToE views quantization itself as a consequence of the entropic field's internal mathematical constraints (such as the Obidi Action and the Obidi Curvature Invariant). In this framework, the continuous entropic field cannot subdivide past a certain point without breaking structural consistency, making Planck's constant ($\hbar$) an emergent threshold rather than an unexplainable starting law. [2, 9]
