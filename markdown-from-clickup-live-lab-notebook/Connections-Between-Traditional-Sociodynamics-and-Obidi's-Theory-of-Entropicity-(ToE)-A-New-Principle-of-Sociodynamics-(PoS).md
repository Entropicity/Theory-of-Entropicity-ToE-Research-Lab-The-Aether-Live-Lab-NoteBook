# Connections Between Traditional Sociodynamics and Obidi's Theory of Entropicity (ToE): A New Principle of Sociodynamics (PoS)

If you are referring to the academic physics and information theory framework, "Obidi's Sociodynamics" does not exist as a standalone formal theory under that specific title. Instead, independent physicist and researcher John Onimisi Obidi is known for pioneering the [Theory of Entropicity (ToE)](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/John-Obidi-4). [1] 
However, if you are looking at how his foundational concepts apply to broader systems—including systemic and social structures—the connection lies in how his physics principles map onto the broader academic definition of sociodynamics (the mathematical and computational modeling of collective human behavior). [2, 3] 
The core mechanics of his framework and how they interact with systemic dynamics include the following:
## 1. The Theory of Entropicity (ToE)
John Onimisi Obidi’s primary work focuses on a paradigm-shifting approach to physics where entropy is treated as the fundamental, primary physical field of reality rather than a derived or secondary property. [1, 4] 

* The Informational Substrate: Grounded in the principle that information is inherently physical, his theory posits that every point in a given system carries a local entropic state. [4] 
* The Obidi Action: Using what is mathematically defined as the Local and Spectral Obidi Action, his research unifies thermodynamics, general relativity, and quantum mechanics. It demonstrates that space, time, and macroscopic dynamics naturally emerge from an underlying statistical-information manifold. [1, 4, 5, 6] 

## 2. Conceptual Bridge to Sociodynamics
While standard [sociodynamics modeling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_dynamics) relies on tools like statistical mechanics and mean-field approximations to predict aggregate human choices and non-equilibrium behaviors over time, Obidi's framework takes the foundational math a step deeper: [3, 7] 

* Irreversible Dynamics: ToE relies heavily on irreversible constraints and the finite rate of entropic rearrangement. In social applications, this mirrors how cultural changes, economic shifts, and community interactions move forward in an asymmetric, irreversible timeline. [3, 6, 8] 
* Systemic Interconnectedness: Because the theory frames all geometry and structural relationships as emerging from informational degrees of freedom, it provides a highly abstract, mathematical parallel to how complex individual social networks aggregate into rigid societal frameworks. [3, 4, 5]
