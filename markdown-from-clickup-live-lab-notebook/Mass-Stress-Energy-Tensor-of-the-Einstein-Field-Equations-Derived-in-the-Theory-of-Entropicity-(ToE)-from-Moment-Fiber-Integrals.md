# Mass Stress Energy Tensor of the-Einstein Field Equations Derived in the Theory of Entropicity (ToE) from Moment Fiber Integrals

In [Obidi's Theory of Entropicity (ToE)](https://medium.com/@jonimisiobidi/foundations-of-obidis-theory-of-entropicity-toe-conceptual-mathematical-and-physical-pillars-929690e65c55), the right-hand side (RHS) of the Einstein field equations—the stress-energy tensor ($T_{\mu\nu}$)—is derived as the macro-level material expression of underlying microscopic entropic information. [1, 2, 3] 

Rather than treating matter and energy as independent inputs, Obidi applies a geometric fiber structure to information states at each discrete point in spacetime. The derivation relies on three primary conceptual mechanics: [1] 

* **Entropic Density Function:** The local density of entropic information is mapped onto a momentum fiber space as a probability or distribution function, acting as the fundamental source. [1] 
* **The Second Moment Integral:** By taking a fiber integral over the momentum fiber at a specific spacetime point, the mathematical framework computes the second statistical moment of this distribution. [1] 
* **Emergence of the Rank-Two Tensor:** Evaluating this second-moment fiber integral naturally yields a symmetric, rank-two tensor. In the macroscopic limit of the [Obidi Action Variation](https://encyclopedia.pub/entry/59188), this tensor directly maps to the physical distribution of energy, momentum, and stress ($T_{\mu\nu}$). [1, 4] 

Through this method, the LHS represents the geometric curvature of the entropic field, while the RHS represents the condensed, integrated material expression of that exact same field. [1]
