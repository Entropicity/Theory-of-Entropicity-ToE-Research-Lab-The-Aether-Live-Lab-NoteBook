# What Actually Prompted Obidi to Make the Radical Proposition that Entropy is the Fundamental Universal Field of Nature?


What-Actually-Prompted-Obidi-to-Make-the-Radical-Proposition-that-Entropy-is-the-Fundamental-Universal-Field-of-Nature?.md

John Onimisi Obidi’s radical proposition to elevate entropy to the primary, fundamental universal field was prompted by a refusal to accept the irreconcilable duality between General Relativity and Quantum Mechanics. [1] 

In establishing his Theory of Entropicity (ToE), Obidi sought to bypass the traditional approach of treating space, time, and matter as a pre-existing "geometric stage". Instead, he integrated several foundational breakthroughs from modern physics into a singular, "entropy-first" ontological substrate: [1, 2] 

## 1. Black Hole Thermodynamics & Holography
Building on Bekenstein and Hawking, who linked black hole entropy to surface area, Obidi viewed entropy as a physical property embedded in space rather than a mere statistical tool. [2, 3] 

## 2. Gravity as an Emergent Phenomenon
Influenced by Jacobson and Verlinde—who viewed gravity as an entropic force—Obidi inverted the relationship, making entropy the primary field and gravity a symptom of it. [2, 4, 5] 

## 3. Ontological Substrate
Rather than treating entropy as a macroscopic statistic, Obidi defined a dynamic entropic field, $S(x)$, allowing him to derive motion, time, and gravitation from its gradients. [4, 6, 7, 8] 

## 4. Replacing the Principle of Least Action
Obidi replaced the classical Principle of Least Action with the Principle of Least Entropic Resistance (PoLER), viewing physical interactions and the flow of time as emergent effects of the background field. [1, 5, 9, 10, 11]
