# So, we know that there are local and global statements of Entropy from the Second Law Thermodynamics (SLoT). This is what Obidi has tried to accomplish in his Theory of Entropicity (ToE) by creating his local and Spectral actions.

In conventional thermodynamics, the distinction between local and global entropy is strictly an accounting mechanism—we look at the local change in entropy inside an open system versus the total global change of the universe. [1, 2, 3] 

However, we are exactly right about the connection of the dual operative nature of entropy to the Theory of Entropicity (ToE) formulated by John Onimisi Obidi. Obidi takes this local/global duality of entropy and promotes it from a simple accounting trick into the fundamental math that constructs the universe. [2, 4, 5, 6] 

In Obidi's framework and formulation, the universe isn't just a place where entropy happens; reality is an entropic manifold. 

**To bridge the gap between local anomalies (like planets forming) and global realities, Obidi splits his mathematical descriptions into two distinct "actions":** [3, 6, 7, 8] 

## 1. The Local Obidi Action (LOA)

### What it governs: 
This treats entropy as a local, continuous scalar field ($S(x)$) flowing through a specific point in space and time.

### The physical result: 
The local gradients and curvatures of this field dictate how matter moves and clumps together locally. In ToE, things like local gravitational pulling (which creates planets) are just local "entropic pressures" acting within the field. [1, 7, 9] 

## 2. The Spectral Obidi Action (SOA)

### What it governs: 
This looks at the global, macroscopic properties of the entire entropic manifold. It uses an operator-algebraic structure to look at the entire "spectrum" of the system at once. [1, 3, 10] 

### The physical result: 
The Spectral Action ensures global consistency. It guarantees that no matter how much the Local Action bends, shapes, or decreases entropy in one tiny spot, the global geometric framework of the universe remains bound to total entropic evolution. [3, 8] 

## The Duality
Obidi’s core breakthrough in ToE is demonstrating a duality between the two. By using mathematical tools like heat-kernel regularization, his work shows that expanding the global Spectral Action actually reproduces the exact same mathematical terms found in the Local Action. They are two sides of the same coin: the local "bunching up" of order and the global "spreading out" of the field are mathematically bound together. [3, 8]

# Scholium 

We know that all of the cosmos [from the ultra small to the ultra large] obeys the second law of thermodynamics (SLoT)!

Yes, the entire process completely obeys the Second Law of Thermodynamics (SLoT).

It is very common to think that planets forming from a messy cloud of dust violates the SLoT, because the SLoT states that entropy (disorder) always increases in the universe. A neat solar system looks much more ordered than a chaotic cloud of dust.
However, the physics adds up perfectly because of one crucial rule: entropy can decrease locally, as long as it increases even more globally.

## 1. The Solar System is an Open System

### Local Order: 
The SLoT only forbids a closed, isolated system from getting more orderly on its own.

### Energy Flow: 
The forming solar system was not isolated. Massive amounts of gravitational energy and heat were moving through it.

### The Analogy: 
Think of water freezing into a highly ordered ice cube. The ice cube got more ordered (lower entropy), but to do that, your freezer had to dump heat out into your kitchen, making the room's molecules jiggle faster (higher total entropy).

## 2. Gravity Trades Potential Energy for Chaotic Heat

### The Starting State: 
A vast, cold cloud of dust spread across light-years has a massive amount of gravitational potential energy.

### The Collapse: 
As gravity pulls the dust together into dense planets and a burning Sun, that neat potential energy is converted into chaotic kinetic energy and extreme heat.

### The Entropy Payoff: 
While the matter (the rocks and gas) clumped into neat spheres, the system radiated a colossal amount of messy, chaotic infrared heat and light out into the freezing cold of empty space.

## 3. The Math Always Wins
The decrease in entropy from making neat, round planets is a tiny drop in the bucket compared to the massive flood of entropy created by the heat radiating into the universe. The total net entropy of the universe increased dramatically during the formation of the solar system.
