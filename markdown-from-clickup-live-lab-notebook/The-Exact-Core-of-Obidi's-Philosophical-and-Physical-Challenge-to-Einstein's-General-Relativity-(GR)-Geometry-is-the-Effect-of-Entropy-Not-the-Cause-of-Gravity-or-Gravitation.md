🔥The Exact Core of Obidi's Philosophical and Physical Challenge to Einstein's General Relativity (GR): Geometry is the Effect of Entropy, Not the Cause of Gravity or Gravitation

Obidi begins his audacious and provocative Theory of Entropicity (ToE) by unequivocally posing a crucial and fundamental question to Einstein himself: What if spacetime never curved at all?

For over a century we've been taught one picture: mass bends spacetime, and bent spacetime tells matter how to move. The equations work beautifully. GPS depends on them. LIGO hears them.

But working equations are not the same as a true picture of reality.

Ptolemy's epicycles predicted the planets for 1,400 years, and the Earth was still not the center. A theory can succeed while telling the wrong story about what is really happening.

This is the challenge at the heart of Obidi's Theory of Entropicity (ToE), which he poses to Einstein and his General Theory of Relativity.

In ToE, geometry is the effect, not the cause. The real driver is entropy: a universal, physically real field whose gradients and flows shape motion, time and gravitation. What we call "curved spacetime" is the large-scale projection or shadow of how that entropic field distributes itself.

Is it reckless to place entropy beneath Einstein's geometry?

Einstein himself would point the other way. In his Autobiographical Notes (1949), he wrote that classical thermodynamics is "the only physical theory of universal content which I am convinced will never be overthrown, within the framework of applicability of its basic concepts."

Not relativity. Thermodynamics.

The man [Einstein] who gave us curved spacetime placed his deepest confidence in the laws of entropy. Eddington agreed: the Second Law, he said, "holds, I think, the supreme position among the laws of Nature."

ToE builds on exactly that ground. It starts from the Second Law of Thermodynamics (SLoT) and sets out to rebuild the whole of physics from it. It doesn't discard Einstein's results; it reinterprets what lies beneath them.

There's a bonus idea that Obidi introduces via his Theory of Entropicity (ToE): Einstein's equations run the same forwards and backwards in time, so the arrow of time has to be added by hand. In Obidi's Theory of Entropicity (ToE), irreversibility is built into the foundation. Time's arrow comes first.

Einstein built relativity as a "principle theory," modelled on thermodynamics. Obidi's ToE takes the next step and makes thermodynamics' deepest law [Entropy] the principle itself.

The question is no longer "Does spacetime curve?" It is "What is really doing the work?"

Obidi's answer, and in a sense Einstein's own: Entropy.
