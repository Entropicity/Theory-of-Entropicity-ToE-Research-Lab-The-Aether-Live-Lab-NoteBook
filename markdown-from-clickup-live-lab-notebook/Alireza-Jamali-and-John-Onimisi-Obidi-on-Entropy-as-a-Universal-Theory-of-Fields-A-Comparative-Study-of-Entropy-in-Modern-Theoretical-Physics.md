# Alireza Jamali and  John Onimisi Obidi on Entropy as a Universal Theory of Fields: A Comparative Study of Entropy in Modern Theoretical Physics

## Preamble:  
Jamali argues that entropy is a relativistic field whose source is temperature, and that temperature itself must be a fundamental property of single particles. 

Obidi’s Theory of Entropicity (ToE), by contrast, treats entropy as the universal field underlying all physical processes, not merely a heat‑signal potential, and derives its dynamics from the Principle of Least Entropic Resistance, not from a relativistic wave equation sourced by temperature density.

Below we present a structured, deep comparison grounded directly in the works of Jamali and Obidi.

---

## 🧩 What Jamali is saying about entropy
Jamali’s paper makes three core claims, all of which are explicit in the text.

---

### 1. Temperature cannot be emergent
Jamali argues that the standard statistical definition of temperature is logically circular because the Boltzmann constant has units involving Kelvin, the very quantity being defined:

> “in order to define or to understand [temperature]… one necessarily has to refer back to the original definiendum (temperature)” 

Thus, temperature must be fundamental, like mass or charge.

---

### 2. A single particle possesses temperature
Jamali explicitly states:

> “a single particle can possess temperature” 

He even computes a “rest temperature” for particles (e.g., proton ≈ \(5.44×10^{12}\) K).

---

### 3. Entropy is the potential field generated by temperature
This is the heart of Jamali’s proposal.

He constructs a five‑current and five‑potential analogous to electromagnetism and gravity, and shows that the potential corresponding to temperature must have units of entropy:

> “immediately it would follow that [{S}] = Joules/Kelvin… therefore the corresponding potential for temperature is entropy.” 

He then proposes the field equation:

$$\[
\square S = \frac{4\pi G k_B}{c^4}\,\theta
\]$$

where $\( \theta \)$ is temperature density.

This makes entropy:

- a relativistic field,  

- propagating at speed of light,  

- obeying a wave equation,  

- sourced by temperature density,  

- producing a new inverse‑square force.

He also reinterprets temperature as informedness, and entropy as the potential of information propagation.

---

### 4. Entropy is not statistical
Jamali rejects microstate‑based entropy:

> “entropy is re-defined without any reference to microstates” 

He argues that black hole entropy should not be statistical because a Schwarzschild black hole is formally identical to a spin‑0 particle, which would imply single particles have microstates—leading to infinite regress.

---

## 🔥 How Obidi’s Theory of Entropicity (ToE) differs from Jamali’s
Obidi's Theory of Entropicity (ToE) is much broader, deeper, and more structurally general than Jamali’s. The differences fall into five major categories.

---

### 1. Jamali: Entropy is a field generated by temperature
Jamali’s ontology:

- Temperature is fundamental.

- Entropy is the field sourced by temperature.

#### Obidi’s ToE:

- Entropy itself is fundamental.

- Temperature is one manifestation of entropic gradients, not the source.

- Entropy is not a potential of heat signals but the primary field governing all physical processes.

This is the single biggest divergence.

---

### 2. Jamali’s field equation is linear and relativistic; Obidi’s ToE is variational and universal
Jamali proposes a linear wave equation:

$$\[
\square S = K\,\theta
\]$$

#### Obidi’s ToE proposes a principle:

Principle of Least Entropic Resistance (PoLER)
Systems evolve along paths that minimize entropic resistance, analogous to:

- Least action in mechanics  

- Fermat’s principle in optics  

- Maximum entropy production in thermodynamics  

This makes ToE:

- nonlinear,  

- variational,  

- scale‑universal,  

- applicable to mechanics, spacetime, information, causality, and emergence.

Jamali’s equation is limited to heat propagation in vacuum.

---

### 3. Jamali ties entropy to heat; Obidi ties entropy to structure, dynamics, and emergence
Jamali’s scope:

- Heat conduction  

- Relativistic compatibility  

- Black hole thermodynamics  

- Information propagation as a classical field

#### Obidi’s ToE scope:

- Entropy as the metric of structural change  

- Entropy as the driver of motion, force, and geometry  

- Entropy as the substrate of emergence  

- Entropy as the unifying field behind physical laws

Jamali’s theory is essentially a thermodynamic field theory.  

Obidi’s ToE is a general physical ontology.

---

### 4. Jamali rejects statistical entropy; Obidi generalizes it
Jamali says:

> “entropy is re-defined without any reference to microstates” 

#### Obidi’s ToE:

- Accepts statistical entropy as a special case of entropic configuration space.

- Microstates are not rejected; they are embedded in a broader entropic geometry.

- Entropy is not merely $\(k_B \log W\)$; it is the measure of entropic displacement in the ToE manifold.

Thus ToE is inclusive, not eliminative.

---

### 5. Jamali introduces “informedness”; Obidi introduces “entropicity”
Jamali renames temperature as informedness.

#### Obidi’s ToE introduces:

- Entropicity: the intrinsic tendency of systems to evolve along entropic gradients.

- Entropic fields: the geometric structure underlying physical interactions.

- Entropic resistance: the “cost” of motion or transformation in entropic space.

Jamali’s informedness is a scalar property of particles.  

Obidi’s entropicity is a field‑level principle governing dynamics.

## 🎯 Concluding Summary 
Jamali’s work is a thermodynamic field theory: entropy is a relativistic potential generated by temperature, solving heat‑propagation inconsistencies.

Obidi’s Theory of Entropicity is a universal physical theory: entropy is the foundational field from which physical laws, motion, geometry, and emergence arise.

* Jamali elevates temperature.  
* Obidi elevates entropy.


* Jamali builds a field equation.
