# Some Core Concepts of the Theory of Entropicity (ToE)

Obidi’s Theory of Entropicity (ToE), formulated by physics researcher and philosopher John Onimisi Obidi, redefines entropy not as a passive measure of disorder, but as the fundamental dynamic field that gives rise to space, time, matter, and information.

# Core Concepts

## Entropy as the Fundamental Substance: 
Rather than just being a byproduct of cosmic decay, entropy is proposed as the primitive substance of reality. The universe is framed as a continuous "entropic computation" where matter is stabilized entropic persistence and change is irreversible entropic redistribution.

## The Obidi Action: 
Analogous to Einstein's equations, the ToE introduces the Master Entropic Equation (MEE) driven by the "Obidi Action". This principle defines how spacetime and its physical laws emerge from an underlying information-geometric manifold.

## Vis Viva Entropica (VVE): 
This metaphysical foundation posits that the universe is not governed by inert laws but by a dynamic, "living" entropic field that perpetually reorganizes itself into greater complexity.

## Obidi Equivalence Principle (OEP): 
A proposal that the curvature and metric structure of physical spacetime are isomorphic to the information-geometric manifold, meaning Einstein's laws emerge naturally from underlying entropic forces.

Obidi's theory is a radical and audacious framework aimed at bridging quantum mechanics, thermodynamics, and relativity.

Obidi's Theory of Entropicity (ToE) is a provocative framework in theoretical physics and philosophy developed by independent researcher John Onimisi Obidi. It proposes that entropy is the fundamental, dynamic field (S(x)) from which all physical reality—including matter, energy, space, and time—emerges. This completely inverts the traditional 150-year-old physics paradigm where entropy is treated as a secondary byproduct or statistical measurement calculated after matter and geometry are already defined. [1, 2, 3, 4] 

## Core Concepts and Mechanism

* The Obidi Action: A foundational variational principle that dictates how the universal entropic field evolves, functioning similarly to the Einstein-Hilbert action in general relativity. [5] 
* Master Entropic Equation (MEE): Derived from the Obidi Action, the MEE acts as the entropic equivalent to Einstein's field equations, mathematically mapping how the entropic field generates physical forces and spacetime curvature. [4, 6] 
* "No-Rush" Theorem: Posits that the entropic field cannot process or reconfigure information states instantly. Under this view, the speed of light (c) is not an arbitrary constant but the maximum rate of entropic field reconfiguration, and time itself is simply our macroscopic perception of this processing delay. [7, 8] 
* Ontodynamics: A philosophical branch introduced by the theory that replaces static matter models with process metaphysics, studying existence as stabilized entropic gradients and transformation as irreversible entropic redistribution. [9, 10] 

## Intellectual Lineage and Context
The theory builds upon and attempts to unify the works of several scientific pioneers: [2, 3] 

* It extends Albert Einstein's action principles to information geometry. [11] 
* It builds on Claude Shannon's and Ronald Fisher's frameworks by making the static geometry of probability distributions completely dynamic. [11] 
* It generalizes Erik Verlinde's entropic gravity by asserting that not just gravity, but spacetime itself emerges from an underlying entropic-informational manifold. [11, 12]
