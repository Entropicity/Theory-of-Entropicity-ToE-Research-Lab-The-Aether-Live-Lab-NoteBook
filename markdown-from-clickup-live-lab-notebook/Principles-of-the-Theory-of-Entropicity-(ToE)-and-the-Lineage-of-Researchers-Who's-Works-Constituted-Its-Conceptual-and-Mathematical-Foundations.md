# Principles of the Theory of Entropicity (ToE) and the Lineage of Researchers Who's Works Constituted Its Conceptual and Mathematical Foundations 

Obidi's Theory of Entropicity (ToE), proposed by John Onimisi Obidi, redefines entropy as the fundamental physical field and generative substrate of reality rather than a mere measure of disorder. [1, 2] 
## Core Principles

* Entropic Field (S(x,t)): Spacetime, matter, and energy emerge from an underlying, continuous dynamical entropy field. [1] 
* The Obidi Action: A foundational variational principle that optimizes entropic flow and bridges information geometry with physical spacetime. [3, 4] 
* Speed of Light (c): Reinterpreted not as an arbitrary speed limit, but as the maximum finite rate at which the entropic field can reorganize itself. [5] 
* No-Rush Theorem: Asserts that state changes and physical interactions cannot happen instantaneously due to these entropic rate constraints. [5] 

## Theoretical Implications

* Emergent Gravity: Gravitation acts as an entropic pressure driven by local field gradients rather than a fundamental geometric pull.
* Dethroning the Observer: Quantum mechanics' reliance on a privileged observer is replaced by local subsystems bound to pre-computed entropic field dynamics. [1, 3, 5, 6]
