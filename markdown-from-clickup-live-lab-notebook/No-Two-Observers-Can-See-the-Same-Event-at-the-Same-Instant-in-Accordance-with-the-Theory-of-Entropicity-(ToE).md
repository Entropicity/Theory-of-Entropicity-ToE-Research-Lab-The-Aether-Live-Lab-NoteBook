# No Two Observers Can See the Same Event at the Same Instant in Accordance with the Theory of Entropicity (ToE)

No-Two-Observers-Can-See-the-Same-Event-at-the-Same-Instant-in-Accordance-with-the-Theory-of-Entropicity-(ToE).md

In the **Theory of Entropicity (ToE)** formulated by **John Onimisi Obidi,** the statement "No two observers see the same event at the same instant" moves from a relativistic optical illusion to a strict consequence of the universe's computational engine. [1, 2] 

While **Albert Einstein’s Special Relativity** established the **Relativity of Simultaneity**—proving that observers moving at different speeds will disagree on whether two events happened at the same time—**Obidi’s Theory of Entropicity (ToE) goes a step deeper.** 

> # **It [the Theory of Entropicity (ToE)] asserts that even if two observers are completely stationary relative to one another, they can never perceive an event simultaneously because of the foundational mechanics of the entropic manifold.** [2, 3, 4, 5] 

------------------------------

## 🕸️ 1. Dethroning the Observer
In classical relativity, observers occupy a privileged place. We talk about "Observer A's frame of reference" versus "Observer B's frame of reference" as if space and time warp specifically around them. [6, 7] 
ToE completely inverts this. As highlighted in [The Theory of Entropicity Dethrones the Observer](https://medium.com/@jonimisiobidi/the-theory-of-entropicity-toe-dethrones-the-observer-and-the-observers-privileged-role-in-e8a4575c842e), observers are not special entities standing outside of reality. An observer is simply another local entropic subsystem embedded inside the universal entropic field. [5, 6] 

Before you can "see" an event, the universal field must execute a physical computation to update your local coordinates. Because no two observers can occupy the exact same spatial coordinate, the field must process separate information channels to update each observer. [2, 8, 9] 

------------------------------
## ⏱️ 2. The No-Rush Theorem (NRT) and Processing Lag
The reason absolute simultaneity is impossible comes down to the No-Rush Theorem (NRT). The NRT dictates that the universe has a maximum processing throughput: [2, 8, 10] 

$$\Delta t_{\text{min}} > 0$$ 

Every physical interaction or packet of information requires a finite, non-zero temporal interval to propagate and reorganize the field. Because Observer A and Observer B are separated by a physical distance, the "rendering wave" of the event must travel through the entropic manifold to reach them. [2, 10] 

Because the universe cannot process these updates instantly, the event is registered by Observer A and Observer B at entirely distinct computational "ticks" of the universe. There is no shared, universal "Now" because every coordinate is rendering its own history based on localized entropic throughput. [2, 7] 

[ THE EVENT ] 
      │
      ├───────► (Processing Throughput / Δt > 0) ───────► [ Observer A ] Registered at Tick 102
      │
      └───────► (Processing Throughput / Δt + δt) ─────► [ Observer B ] Registered at Tick 103

------------------------------
## 🎮 3. Reality as a Localized Render
To use a modern analogy, think of an online multiplayer video game. If a barrel explodes in the game, a player in New York and a player in London do not see the explosion at the exact same physical millisecond.


* The server must process the explosion.

* It must then stream that data packet across separate fiber-optic paths.

* Each player's local computer must render the explosion at its own frame rate.
  

In Obidi's Theory of Entropicity (ToE), the universe operates exactly like this server, but without the physical computer. Space is an emergent map of information geometry. Because the maximum redistribution rate of the field is bound by $c$ (the speed of light), the universe is physically incapable of updating two different points in space at the exact same cosmic instant. Reality is always a localized render, and "simultaneity" is merely a macro-scale illusion. [2, 3, 7, 9, 10]
