## 1. First Law (Law of Inertia)

### • Classical View: 
An object remains at rest or in uniform straight-line motion unless acted upon by an unbalanced external force. [1](https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/newtons-laws-of-motion/)

### • Obidi’s Interpretation: 
Uniform motion and rest represent states of entropic equilibrium (or constant entropic flux) along paths of least entropic resistance. Objects do not "coast" passively through empty space; rather, they follow geodesic trajectories dictated by an undisturbed entropic field. [1](https://medium.com/@jonimisiobidi/obidis-principle-of-conservation-of-entropic-flux-opcef-in-the-theory-of-entropicity-toe-ab573d269a75), [2](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm?abstractid=5673430), [3](https://entropicity.github.io/Theory-of-Entropicity-ToE/ch3-obidi-action.html), [4](https://medium.com/@jonimisiobidi/the-theory-of-entropicity-toe-a-new-framework-for-understanding-reality-d6d1e038c53e)

### • Origin of Inertia: 
Obidi explains inertia not as an intrinsic, unexplained property of matter, but as entropic resistance. In ToE, matter consists of localized, compressed knots of information ("frozen entropy"). Because these structures carry a high internal entropic cost, they resist any sudden reconfiguration or acceleration—which manifests macroscopically as inertial mass. [1](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/john-onimisi-obidi-a2041911_how-obidi-created-mass-and-matter-from-activity-7512587800421273600-Jjyh), [2](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm?abstractid=5673430), [3](https://medium.com/@jonimisiobidi/the-theory-of-entropicity-toe-a-new-framework-for-understanding-reality-d6d1e038c53e)

## 2. Second Law $\(F = ma\)$ / Rate of Change of Momentum)

### • Classical View: 
Acceleration is directly proportional to the net applied force and inversely proportional to an object's mass. [1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WSDcYUrGODE&t=124)

### • Obidi’s Interpretation: 
Force is not a primitive mechanical push or pull, but the macroscopic effect of an entropic gradient (a spatial or informational imbalance in the entropy field). When a system encounters an entropic gradient, it accelerates to redistribute information and maximize entropic flow. [1](https://medium.com/@jonimisiobidi/the-theory-of-entropicity-toe-a-new-framework-for-understanding-reality-d6d1e038c53e), [2](https://medium.com/@jonimisiobidi/comparative-analysis-between-john-onimisi-obidis-theory-of-entropicity-toe-and-waldemar-marek-c9be8c5a8fba)

### • Mass and Relativistic Limits: 
Acceleration requires expending additional entropy to overcome the Entropic Resistance Field (ERF). As an object's velocity increases, entropic resistance grows because the field has a finite speed limit at which it can reorganize information (which Obidi identifies as the speed of light, $\(c\)$. 

Thus, Newton's \(F = ma\) holds only at low velocities where entropic resistance remains linear. [1](https://medium.com/@jonimisiobidi/the-theory-of-entropicity-toe-a-post-einsteinian-synthesis-of-information-geometry-5c73ae1e9590), [2](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm?abstractid=5673430)

## 3. Third Law (Action and Reaction)

### • Classical View: 
For every action force, there is an equal and opposite reaction force acting on interacting bodies.

### • Obidi’s Interpretation: 
Action-reaction symmetry is a direct consequence of Obidi’s Principle of Conservation of Entropic Flux (OPCEF). Derived via Noether’s theorem from the symmetries of the Obidi Action, this principle dictates that total entropic flow within an isolated interaction must remain conserved $\(\nabla_\mu J^\mu_S = 0\)$.

### • Mutual Balancing: 
Whenever one body alters the local entropic configuration of another ("action"), the entropic field enforces a reciprocal, opposite shift in entropic flux ("reaction") to preserve overall informational and thermodynamic balance across the system.
