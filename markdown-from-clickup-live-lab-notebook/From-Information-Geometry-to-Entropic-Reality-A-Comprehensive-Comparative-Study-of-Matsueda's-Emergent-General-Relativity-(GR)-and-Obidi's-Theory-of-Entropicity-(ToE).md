# Abstract

The relationship between entropy, information, probability, geometry, and gravity has become one of the most profound themes in modern theoretical physics. Over the last several decades, multiple approaches have suggested that gravity may not be fundamental but rather an emergent phenomenon arising from deeper informational or entropic structures.

Among these efforts, Hiroaki Matsueda's work on Emergent General Relativity from Fisher Information Metric develops a formal information-geometric derivation of Einstein-like gravity from probability distributions. [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.1831)[2](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1310.1831)

In contrast, John Onimisi Obidi's Theory of Entropicity (ToE) advances a far more expansive ontological program by proposing entropy itself as the fundamental substrate of reality from which geometry, matter, information, spacetime, and physical law emerge. [3](https://entropicity.github.io/Theory-of-Entropicity-ToE/)[4](https://encyclopedia.pub/entry/59188)[5](https://medium.com/@jonimisiobidi/foundations-of-obidis-theory-of-entropicity-toe-500d7a162eee)

This paper explores the mathematical, conceptual, and philosophical distinctions between these two approaches and examines whether the emergence of field-like entropic structures in Matsueda's framework provides indirect support for entropy-first ontologies such as the Theory of Entropicity.

# Introduction

Physics has traditionally regarded entropy as a derived quantity.

In statistical mechanics,

$$
S = k_B \ln W
$$

while in information theory,

$$
H = - \sum_i p_i \log p_i.
$$

In both cases, entropy is usually treated as secondary.

The primary objects are probabilities, microstates, or physical systems.

Entropy merely describes them.

However, a growing collection of theories has questioned this hierarchy.

What if entropy is not merely descriptive?

What if entropy is generative?

What if geometry, spacetime, and gravity arise from entropy itself?

These questions sit at the heart of both Matsueda's and Obidi's investigations, though each pursues dramatically different paths.

---

# The Information-Geometric Program of Matsueda

## The Starting Point: Probability Distributions

Matsueda begins not with spacetime, matter, gravity, or entropy fields.

Instead, the foundation is a family of probability distributions

$$
p(x;\theta).
$$

Here,

$$
x
$$

labels microscopic states and

$$
\theta^\mu
$$

represents parameters characterizing the system. [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.1831)[2](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1310.1831)

The fundamental quantity introduced is

$$
\gamma(x,\theta)=
-\ln p(x;\theta).
$$

This quantity measures local information content or surprisal. [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.1831)[2](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1310.1831)

---

## Constructing the Fisher Information Metric

The next step is the construction of the Fisher metric,

$$
g_{\mu\nu}=
\left\langle
\partial_\mu \gamma
\,
\partial_\nu \gamma
\right\rangle.
$$

Equivalently,

$$
g_{\mu\nu}=
\sum_x
p(x;\theta)
\,
\partial_\mu \ln p
\,
\partial_\nu \ln p.
$$

This metric measures distinguishability between neighboring probability distributions. [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.1831)[2](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1310.1831)

The remarkable move occurs here.

The statistical manifold is reinterpreted as a geometric manifold.

Probability becomes geometry.

---

## Geometry Emerges from Information

Once the metric exists,

$$
ds^2=
g_{\mu\nu}
d\theta^\mu
d\theta^\nu
$$

defines a line element.

The manifold is now equipped with all standard structures of differential geometry. [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.1831)[2](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1310.1831)

The Christoffel symbols become

$$
\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu}=
\frac12
g^{\lambda\rho}
\left(
\partial_\mu g_{\rho\nu}+
\partial_\nu g_{\rho\mu}-
\partial_\rho g_{\mu\nu}
\right).
$$

The Riemann curvature tensor becomes

$$
R^\rho_{\sigma\mu\nu}=
\partial_\mu \Gamma^\rho_{\nu\sigma}-
\partial_\nu \Gamma^\rho_{\mu\sigma}+
\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\lambda}
\Gamma^\lambda_{\nu\sigma}-
\Gamma^\rho_{\nu\lambda}
\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\sigma}.
$$

The Ricci tensor is

$$
R_{\mu\nu}=
R^\lambda_{\mu\lambda\nu}.
$$

The Ricci scalar is

$$
R=
g^{\mu\nu}
R_{\mu\nu}.
$$

Finally,

$$
G_{\mu\nu}=
R_{\mu\nu}-
\frac12
g_{\mu\nu}
R.
$$

The Einstein tensor emerges. [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.1831)[2](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1310.1831)

# The Surprising Appearance of an Entropy-Like Field

## The Emergent Scalar Structure

Matsueda observes that when curvature is expressed in terms of information-theoretic quantities, the resulting Einstein tensor naturally contains terms of the form

$$
\partial_\mu \psi\,
\partial_\nu \psi-\frac12
g_{\mu\nu}
(\partial \psi)^2.
$$

This structure is immediately recognizable.

It is mathematically identical to the stress-energy tensor of a classical scalar field,

$$
T_{\mu\nu}=
\partial_\mu \phi
\,
\partial_\nu \phi-
\frac12
g_{\mu\nu}
(\partial \phi)^2.
$$

Consequently,

$$
G_{\mu\nu}
\sim
T_{\mu\nu}^{(\psi)}.
$$

The entropy-related information potential behaves exactly like a scalar field sourcing gravity. [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.1831)[2](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1310.1831)

## Did Matsueda Discover an Entropic Field?

This question touches the philosophical heart of the debate.

Strictly speaking, Matsueda does not begin with a field.

He begins with probabilities.

The field appears later.

His logical sequence is

$$
p
\rightarrow
\gamma
\rightarrow
g_{\mu\nu}
\rightarrow
G_{\mu\nu}.
$$

The entropy-like field emerges from information geometry. [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.1831)[2](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1310.1831)

Yet its emergence raises a profound question.

If an entropy-related quantity inevitably manifests itself as a field-like entity capable of sourcing gravity, might entropy possess a deeper ontological status than originally assumed?

This question leads naturally to the Theory of Entropicity.

Entropy ceases to be a descriptive quantity.

It becomes the primary ontological field. [3](https://entropicity.github.io/Theory-of-Entropicity-ToE/)[5](https://medium.com/@jonimisiobidi/foundations-of-obidis-theory-of-entropicity-toe-500d7a162eee)

# The Crucial Difference Between the Two Fields

## Matsueda's Field

Matsueda's field is fundamentally informational.

Its origin lies in

$$
p(x;\theta).
$$

From this,

$$
\gamma
=
-\ln p
$$

is constructed.

Only afterward does field-like behavior emerge. [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.1831)[2](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1310.1831)

Thus,

$$
p
\rightarrow
\gamma
\rightarrow
\text{Field-like Dynamics}.
$$

The field is emergent.

---

## Obidi's Field

In the Theory of Entropicity, the Entropic Field is not derived.

It is postulated as primary. [3](https://entropicity.github.io/Theory-of-Entropicity-ToE/)[5](https://medium.com/@jonimisiobidi/foundations-of-obidis-theory-of-entropicity-toe-500d7a162eee)

The conceptual flow is

$$
S
\rightarrow
p
\rightarrow
\text{Information}
\rightarrow
\text{Geometry}.
$$

Probability itself becomes secondary.

The field is fundamental.

# Shannon Entropy and the Entropic Question

An important observation arises from Shannon's definition,

$$
H=
-\sum_i p_i \log p_i.
$$

Notice that entropy is a functional of probability.

If

$$
p=p(x),
$$

then

$$
H=H(x).
$$

Entropy automatically acquires field-like behavior.

This fact creates a subtle tension.

One may argue that entropy becoming field-like is simply a consequence of probability being spatially varying.

On the other hand, one may argue that the appearance of field-like entropy across multiple contexts indicates a deeper ontological significance.

This is precisely where Matsueda's framework and the Theory of Entropicity diverge.

---

# Epistemic Versus Ontic Entropy

The distinction may be expressed through the language of philosophy.

For Matsueda,

entropy is primarily epistemic.

It quantifies uncertainty, distinguishability, and information content.

It tells us something about states. [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.1831)[2](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1310.1831)

For the Theory of Entropicity,

entropy is ontic.

It is proposed as something that exists independently and physically.

It constitutes reality itself. [3](https://entropicity.github.io/Theory-of-Entropicity-ToE/)[5](https://medium.com/@jonimisiobidi/foundations-of-obidis-theory-of-entropicity-toe-500d7a162eee)

This difference cannot be overstated.

The two theories are not merely different mathematically.

They are different in what they believe the universe fundamentally consists of.

---

# Gravity Versus Reality

The scope of the two programs differs dramatically.

Matsueda asks:

$$
\text{Can gravity emerge from information geometry?}
$$

His work remains closely tied to Einstein's General Relativity. [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.1831)[2](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1310.1831)

The Theory of Entropicity asks:

$$
\text{Can reality emerge from entropy?}
$$

This includes gravity but extends beyond it toward information, causality, spacetime, quantum theory, and ontology itself. [3](https://entropicity.github.io/Theory-of-Entropicity-ToE/)[5](https://medium.com/@jonimisiobidi/foundations-of-obidis-theory-of-entropicity-toe-500d7a162eee)[4](https://encyclopedia.pub/entry/59188)

One framework is primarily gravitational.

The other aspires to be foundational.

---

# A Possible Historical Interpretation

Viewed historically, Matsueda's work may be interpreted as part of the growing movement toward informational foundations of physics.

His achievement is demonstrating that Einstein-like geometry can arise directly from information geometry. [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.1831)[2](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1310.1831)

The Theory of Entropicity may be viewed as a further philosophical step.

Rather than accepting information as primary, it asks whether entropy itself should occupy the deepest layer of reality. [3](https://entropicity.github.io/Theory-of-Entropicity-ToE/)[5](https://medium.com/@jonimisiobidi/foundations-of-obidis-theory-of-entropicity-toe-500d7a162eee)

In this reading, Matsueda explores how information becomes gravity.

Obidi explores how entropy becomes everything.

# Conclusion

The comparison between Matsueda's emergent gravity and Obidi's Theory of Entropicity reveals two distinct visions of the informational universe.

Matsueda begins with probability distributions,

$$
p(x;\theta),
$$

constructs the Fisher metric,

$$
g_{\mu\nu},
$$

and derives Einstein-like gravitational dynamics. In this process, entropy-related quantities emerge as field-like structures capable of sourcing curvature. [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.1831)[2](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1310.1831)

The Theory of Entropicity begins with a fundamentally different assumption. Entropy is elevated from a derivative measure to a primary field,

$$
S(x),
$$

from which information, geometry, matter, and gravity emerge. [3](https://entropicity.github.io/Theory-of-Entropicity-ToE/)[5](https://medium.com/@jonimisiobidi/foundations-of-obidis-theory-of-entropicity-toe-500d7a162eee)

Thus, the deepest contrast is not whether entropy matters.

Both frameworks place entropy near the center of physics.

The real difference concerns hierarchy.

Matsueda derives gravity from information.

Obidi seeks to derive information itself from entropy.

Matsueda investigates the emergence of General Relativity.

Obidi ventures into deeper ontological waters, asking whether entropy is the foundational substance from which reality itself unfolds.
