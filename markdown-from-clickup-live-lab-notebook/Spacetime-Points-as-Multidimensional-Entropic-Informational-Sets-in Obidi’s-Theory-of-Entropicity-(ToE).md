# Spacetime Points as Multidimensional Entropic–Informational Sets in Obidi’s Theory of Entropicity (ToE)


Spacetime-Points-as-Multidimensional-Entropic-Informational-Sets-in Obidi’s-Theory-of-Entropicity-(ToE).md


In the Theory of Entropicity, Obidi rejects the classical assumption that a point in spacetime is a primitive geometric atom—an infinitesimal location with no internal structure. Classical physics treats spacetime points as featureless coordinates, mere labels in a manifold. They are assumed to be ontologically empty, possessing no internal degrees of freedom. This assumption is inherited from Euclidean geometry, carried into differential geometry, and preserved in general relativity. A point is simply a location, nothing more.

Obidi argues that this view is fundamentally incomplete. In ToE, a spacetime point is not a geometric primitive but an entropic–informational primitive. It is not a location in a pre‑existing geometric container; it is a local entropic state of the underlying informational substrate. Each point carries a definite entropic value \(S(x)\), and this value is not decorative or secondary. It is the actual content of the point. The point exists because the entropic field assigns it a distinguishable informational state. Without entropy, there is no distinguishability; without distinguishability, there is no geometry; without geometry, there is no spacetime.

Thus, a spacetime point in ToE is not a simple coordinate. It is a multidimensional, multifaceted entropic informational set. It contains the local entropic density, the local entropic gradient, the local informational curvature, and the local dual entropic potential generated through Legendre–Fenchel conjugacy. These quantities together define the “state” of the point. The point is therefore a structured informational entity, not an empty geometric placeholder.

This reconceptualization has profound consequences. Because each point is an entropic informational set, the geometry of spacetime is not fundamental; it is emergent. The metric \(g_{\mu\nu}(x)\) is not imposed on the manifold; it is derived from the entropic structure of the points themselves. The curvature of spacetime is the curvature of the entropic field. The causal structure of spacetime is the organization of entropic information flow. The speed of light is the maximal rate at which these entropic informational sets can update relative to one another. In this view, spacetime is not a stage on which entropy evolves; spacetime is the global arrangement of entropic states.

This also explains why light has an invariant speed for all observers. Because each spacetime point is an entropic informational set, the observer’s clocks, rulers, and measurement procedures are themselves entropic processes embedded in the same substrate. When an observer moves, the entropic configuration of their local informational sets changes. Their clocks tick differently, their rulers contract, and their simultaneity relations shift. These changes are not arbitrary; they are entropic compensations that preserve the maximal update rate of the substrate. Light is the mode that saturates this rate, and because the observer’s measuring apparatus is constrained by the same entropic limit, all observers measure the same speed for light. Ordinary objects, which do not saturate the entropic limit, do not enjoy this invariance. Their measured speeds vary because their motion does not probe the maximal informational update rate of the substrate.

Thus, Obidi’s claim is not merely that spacetime points contain entropy. His claim is that the point is the entropy. The point is the informational state. The point is the entropic event. Spacetime is the manifold of these entropic informational sets, and its geometry is the emergent relational structure among them. This is the conceptual leap that distinguishes ToE from classical physics: the recognition that the fundamental units of reality are not geometric but entropic, not spatial but informational, not empty but structured.
