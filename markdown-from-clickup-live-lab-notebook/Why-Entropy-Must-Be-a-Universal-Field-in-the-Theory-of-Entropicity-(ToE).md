# Why Entropy Must Be a Universal Field in the Theory of Entropicity (ToE): Albert Einstein, Bekenstein-Hawking, Ted Jacobson, Thanu Padmanabhan, Ariel Caticha, Erik Verlinde, Ginestra Bianconi

The objection that entropy cannot be a field targets the primary conceptual leap of the theory. In standard, textbook physics, entropy is a macroscopic property of a system (a single number, like 50 J/K) or a statistical count of microstates, not a continuous field that changes from point to point in space. [1, 2] 
To understand why the theory proposes this, it helps to look at how it redefines the concept to argue against the traditional view:
## 1. Shifting from Statistical to Informational Fields
Standard physics treats entropy as a secondary property calculated after matter and spacetime are already established. Obidi’s framework attempts to invert this by building on Information Geometry (utilizing frameworks like Fisher Information and Shannon Entropy). [3, 4, 5, 6] 
Instead of treating entropy as a property of physical "stuff," the theory views the universe at its most fundamental level as pure probability distributions. In mathematics, a space of probability distributions can possess its own geometry, metrics, and fields. Under this view, the "entropic field" $S(x)$ is a mathematical description of how information densities change across an informational manifold. [7] 
## 2. The Precedent of Emergent Spacetime
The idea that thermodynamic properties can behave like fields or generate geometry is not entirely unique to this theory. It draws inspiration from established mainstream physics concepts: [8] 

* Entropic Gravity: Physicist Erik Verlinde famously proposed that gravity is not a fundamental force, but an emergent "entropic force" arising from the information stored on holographic screens. [9, 10, 11, 12] 
* Black Hole Thermodynamics: Jacob Bekenstein and Stephen Hawking demonstrated that black holes have a well-defined entropy proportional to their surface area, directly linking thermodynamic entropy to geometric space. [13, 14, 15] 

## 3. The Core Scientific Debate
From a strict, mainstream physics perspective, the skepticism is entirely valid. For the Theory of Entropicity to move from a speculative philosophical framework to an accepted physical theory, it must overcome major hurdles: [16] 

* **The Background Independence Problem:** Standard fields exist inside spacetime. If entropy is the field that creates spacetime, it must be mathematically defined without assuming a pre-existing space or time background.
* **Testable Predictions:** To prove traditional physics wrong, the Master Entropic Equation must predict a measurable physical phenomenon that General Relativity and Quantum Mechanics cannot explain.
