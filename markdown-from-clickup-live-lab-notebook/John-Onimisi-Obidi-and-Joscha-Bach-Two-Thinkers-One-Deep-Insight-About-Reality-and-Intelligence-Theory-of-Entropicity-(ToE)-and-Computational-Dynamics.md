# 🔷 John Onimisi Obidi & Joscha Bach: Two Thinkers, One Deep Insight About Reality and Intelligence from the Lens of the Theory of Entropicity (ToE) and Computational Dynamics 


Over the past few weeks, I’ve been studying two very different intellectual frameworks:  
John Onimisi Obidi’s Theory of Entropicity (ToE) and Joscha Bach’s cognitive‑computational philosophy of mind, consciousness, and artificial intelligence.

What’s remarkable is how strongly these two perspectives converge — even though one is rooted in fundamental physics (John Onimisi Obidi) and the other in computational cognition (Joscha Bach).

Both Obidi and Bach argue that the deepest structures of reality and intelligence do not emerge from geometry, symbols, or surface patterns.  
They emerge from information (which Obidi rigorously constructs as an Entropic Field).

## 🧠 Bach: Intelligence as an Internal Generative Model
Joscha Bach has long emphasized that true intelligence requires:

- an internal world‑model  
- generative structure  
- self‑consistent informational dynamics  
- meaning grounded in internal computation  

He argues that current AI systems are powerful but lack genuine understanding because they operate on correlations rather than internal informational causality.

## 🌌 Obidi: The Universe as an Entropic Information Field
Obidi’s Theory of Entropicity (ToE) makes a parallel claim at the level of physics:

- the universe emerges from a scalar entropic field, S(x)  
- geometry, matter, and physical law arise from information gradients  
- tensors and spacetime are outputs, not primitives  
- reality is fundamentally informational, not geometric  

In ToE, the Fisher‑Rao metric transforms entropic gradients into the curvature of spacetime — meaning the universe itself is a generative informational engine.

## 🔗 The Convergence: Information as Ontology
Despite working in different domains, both thinkers converge on a profound insight:

> Complex structure — whether physical or cognitive — emerges from internal informational dynamics.

This shared foundation leads to striking parallels:

- Both reject surface‑level explanations.  
- Both treat geometry and cognition as emergent.  
- Both emphasize generativity over correlation.  
- Both see information as the substrate of reality.  

Obidi applies this to the universe.  
Bach applies it to the mind.  
Together, they outline a unified informational ontology as a unified vision of emergence across mind, consciousness, and cosmos.
