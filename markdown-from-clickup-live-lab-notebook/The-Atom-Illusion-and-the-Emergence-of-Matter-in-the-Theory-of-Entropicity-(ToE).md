## The "What is an Electron?" Problem:

If you zoom in on an electron, it has no size, no edge, and no surface. It is defined entirely by a few numbers: its mass, its charge, and its spin. In a very literal sense, an electron is just a tiny packet of properties—which is exactly what a piece of data is.

Obidi’s theory simply takes this to its logical conclusion: if everything we call "matter" is just a collection of mathematical properties interacting via forces, then the universe isn't a collection of things; it is a collection of relations.

When you look at a tree, you aren't looking at "wood matter." You are looking at a localized region where the cosmic network is processing a massive, highly ordered stream of information, and your brain decodes that processing speed as a brown, solid trunk. It means we aren't observers standing inside a giant physical room; we are part of the calculation.

## Gravity in the Netflix Analogy: The Path of Least Resistance:
