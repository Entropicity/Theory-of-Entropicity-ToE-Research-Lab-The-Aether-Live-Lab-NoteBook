# On the Sociological and Philosophical Implications of Obidi’s Theory of Entropicity (ToE): From Obidi’s Reframing of What a Point in Spacetime Means to a New Principle of Sociodynamics (PoS)

John Onimisi Obidi’s Theory of Entropicity (ToE) introduces a conceptual shift so fundamental that its implications extend far beyond physics. By redefining what a point in spacetime truly is, Obidi reframes the foundations of ontology, epistemology, social theory, and human identity. His claim is radical yet precise: a point in spacetime is not an empty coordinate but an entropic event, a locus of distinguishability generated by the underlying entropic field. This single insight becomes the seed from which a new sociological and philosophical worldview emerges.

At the heart of ToE is the assertion that entropy is not merely a measure of disorder but the primary ontological substrate of reality. Entropy creates distinguishability; distinguishability creates information; information creates geometry; geometry creates spacetime; and spacetime creates the conditions for matter, energy, and human existence. A point exists only because entropy makes it distinct. Without entropy, there would be no “here,” no “there,” no “you,” no “me,” and no society. This reframing transforms the meaning of existence itself.

Obidi’s description of a spacetime point as a multidimensional entropic–informational set overturns the classical view of spacetime as a passive container. In ToE, spacetime is a macro‑level projection of underlying entropic states. A point is not a location; it is a distinguishable informational configuration. Much like multiple radio frequencies coexisting in the same region, each spacetime point contains layered informational modes. We perceive a single point only because our cognitive apparatus selects one coherent mode from this entropic spectrum. This is the informational‑entropic dual: each point carries both a local entropic state [S(x)] and an informational configuration [I(x)], governed by metrics such as Fisher–Rao.

This reconceptualization has profound philosophical consequences. Classical metaphysics treats entities as isolated substances. Obidi’s ToE dissolves this isolation. A point cannot exist without contrast; relation precedes existence. The “I” and the “you” are not independent beings but distinguishable entropic events within the same field. Identity becomes a gradient, not a boundary. The mind–matter divide collapses because both are expressions of informational structure. Reality becomes a continuous process of entropic becoming rather than a static collection of objects.

The sociological implications are equally transformative. If individuals, communities, and institutions are entropic events within a social field, then society itself becomes a dynamic entropic manifold. Social cohesion arises from distinguishability, not uniformity. Hyper-conformity destroys the entropic gradients necessary for a functioning society, leading to stagnation or collapse. Diversity becomes an entropic necessity, not a cultural preference. Human networks behave like entropic fields: when information is overly centralized, bottlenecks form, slowing societal motion. When constraints become too dense, systems undergo entropic release—revolutions, reforms, or structural reorganization.

Obidi’s reframing also reshapes our understanding of human agency. Consciousness becomes a localized engine of entropic transformation, a mechanism by which the entropic field observes and navigates its own informational gradients. Human creativity, decision-making, and social interaction are entropic processes that increase distinguishability within the social manifold. In this sense, society evolves through entropic motion: individuals and groups transition through distinguishable states, forming trajectories analogous to physical motion in spacetime.

This leads to **a new principle of sociodynamics (PoS):** social motion is the traversal of entropic distinguishability. A society progresses when its members can move from state A to state B—economically, intellectually, politically—because entropy creates the necessary gradients. When these gradients are suppressed, motion ceases, and the society enters a state of entropic stagnation. Borders, institutions, and cultural norms become informational constraints shaping the geometry of social spacetime.
