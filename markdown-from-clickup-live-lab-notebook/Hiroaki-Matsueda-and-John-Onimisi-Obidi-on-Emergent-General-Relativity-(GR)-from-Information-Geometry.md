The 2013 paper you are referring to is titled ["Emergent General Relativity from Fisher Information Metric"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.1831) by the Japanese physicist Hiroaki Matsueda. [1, 2] 

This work serves as a vital mathematical bridge and historical antecedent to modern "gravity from information" frameworks, including Obidi's Theory of Entropicity (ToE). [3, 4] 

## The Core Premise of the Paper
Matsueda demonstrates that Einstein's Field Equations are not fundamental geometric postulates, but rather macroscopic equations governing coarse-grained information states. [2, 3] 

Instead of starting with a physical spacetime fabric, Matsueda starts with pure information theory:

   ### 1. The Substrate: 
   He begins with the probability distribution of a microscopic statistical mechanical system (or quantum entanglement data). [2, 5] 
   
   ### 2. The Geometry: 
   He builds a mathematical coordinate space out of these probabilities using the Fisher Information Metric—a standard tool in information geometry used to measure how distinguishable two probability states are. [2, 6] 
   
   ### 3. The Curvature: 
   By calculating the geometric curvature of this informational map, Matsueda mathematically derives an Einstein-type curvature tensor directly out of the Fisher metric. [2, 4] 

## How Matter and Gravity Emerge
In Einstein’s relativity, spacetime is curved by physical mass and energy (represented by the energy-momentum tensor). In Matsueda’s 2013 paper, this relationship is flipped:


### * Entropy as Sourced Energy: 
Matsueda shows that when you embed microscopic entropy data or spectrum data into this information space, it naturally behaves exactly like the energy-momentum tensor of a classical scalar field. [2] 

### * Emergent Gravity: 
Consequently, gravity is shown to be an emergent **"entropic force".** The equations that govern how information changes at the microscopic level scale up to become the exact Einstein field equations we see macroscopically. [2, 3] 


## The Connection to Obidi's ToE
Matsueda's paper is highly relevant to John Onimisi Obidi’s framework. Matsueda successfully demonstrated that a Fisher information manifold could yield an Einstein tensor. 

Obidi later builds heavily upon this concept by introducing the Obidi Transformation and Amari–Čencov $\alpha$-connections to bridge the gap between classical information geometry and actual causal, physical Lorentzian spacetimes. [2, 4, 7]

# Scholium 


While Hiroaki Matsueda’s 2013 paper pioneered the concept of deriving General Relativity directly from a Fisher Information Metric, John Onimisi Obidi’s approach introduces radical structural transformations to convert pure information into physical reality. [1] 

Matsueda showed that an information manifold can look like gravity, but Obidi provides the dynamic physical mechanism. [2] 

Their methods differ fundamentally across four major dimensions:

## 1. Static Equilibrium vs. Non-Equilibrium Dynamics


### * Matsueda: 
His 2013 derivation is essentially thermodynamic and time-symmetric. He takes a stationary probability distribution (like quantum entanglement states or microscopic spectrum data) and maps its static properties to show that it mirrors an Einstein-like curvature tensor. [1] 

### * Obidi: 
His framework is irreversible and fully dynamic. In the [Theory of Entropicity (ToE)](https://theory-of-entropicity-toe.pages.dev/), entropy is treated as an active, evolving field governed by a strict variational principle: the Obidi Action. 

Gravity isn't just a passive reflection of information structure; it is driven by active informational rearrangements and entropic flux. [1, 3, 4, 5] 


## 2. Overcoming Čencov’s Theorem (The Metric Type)


### * Matsueda: 
Uses standard classical Information Geometry, which relies heavily on the Fisher Information Metric and the Amari–Čencov $\alpha$-connections. However, classical Fisher metrics are strictly Riemannian (positive-definite metrics like those used for normal spatial distances). They lack an intrinsic temporal direction. [6] 

### * Obidi: 
Introduces the Obidi Transformation, which applies a mathematical "rank-one disformal deformation" to the underlying statistical manifold. This explicitly breaks classical symmetry to convert a purely statistical spatial metric into a Lorentzian metric (the $-+++$ signature required for the actual physical flow of time and causality). [2] 


## 3. Generalized Entropy Functions


### * Matsueda: 
Anchors his mathematics primarily within standard Boltzmann–Gibbs statistics and Shannon entropy limits.

### * Obidi: Broadens the mathematical landscape to a Hybrid Metric-Affine Space (HMAS), utilizing an $\alpha$-q constitutive constraint. This integrates generalized functional deformations, such as Rényi and Tsallis entropies, allowing ToE to calculate highly non-linear, non-equilibrium systems where classical thermodynamics break down. [1, 2] 


## 4. How Einstein is Recovered


### * Matsueda: Macro-level gravity is a direct holographic or coarse-grained translation of micro-level states.

### * Obidi: 
Einstein's Field Equations are a low-gradient, near-equilibrium mathematical limit of Obidi's broader Master Entropic Equation (MEE). In Obidi's framework, Einstein's equations represent a universe at thermodynamic rest; when intense entropic gradients conflict, ToE deviates from General Relativity to predict phenomena without traditional spacetime curvature (such as the anomalous precession of Mercury). [2]
