# Challenges Before Obidi's Principle of Least Entropic Resistance (PoLER) of the Theory of Entropicity (ToE)

Challenges-Before-Obidi's-Principle-of-Least-Entropic-Resistance-(PoLER)-of-the-Theory-of-Entropicity-(ToE).md

Deriving classical dynamics from an independently defined entropic principle is a substantial foundational research problem. My suggestion to begin with the harmonic oscillator may have made the task sound easier than it is. The oscillator is a simple system to describe; deriving its dynamics from a new foundation can be extremely difficult.

The difficulty lies in establishing several connections simultaneously:

## Meaning:
What precisely is entropic resistance, and what physical quantity would measure it?

## Dynamics: 
Why should that resistance determine motion?

## Mathematics: 
Does the proposed optimization produce consistent, sufficiently well-defined equations?

## Correspondence: 
How do reversible motion, inertia, oscillation, and conservation emerge?

## Evidence: 
What would distinguish the entropic explanation from an alternative description of the same observations?


The deepest obstacle is that entropy alone generally does not specify the rate or route of evolution. A theory must explain how its entropic structure supplies that missing dynamical information. If the needed dynamics are inserted through assumptions, those assumptions must be acknowledged; otherwise, the derivation can inadvertently assume what it seeks to explain.

That makes it important to distinguish three achievements:

### * Conceiving PoLER is proposing a physical organizing principle. 

### * Formulating PoLER is giving that principle precise mathematical content. 

### * Establishing PoLER is demonstrating its consequences and testing whether they describe nature. 

These are different stages of a research program, and the first does not automatically accomplish the others.

The appropriate next objective is therefore narrower than deriving all mechanics: construct one explicit model in which entropic resistance is independently defined, explain the assumptions behind it, and determine what motion actually follows. Even a restricted result—or a clear demonstration that a particular formulation fails—would provide useful progress.

The general sense that the undertaking is formidable is accurate. 

* Its difficulty neither validates nor discredits PoLER.
* What matters is whether each step makes the proposal **more precise, less dependent on unstated assumptions, and more open to calculation and testing.**
