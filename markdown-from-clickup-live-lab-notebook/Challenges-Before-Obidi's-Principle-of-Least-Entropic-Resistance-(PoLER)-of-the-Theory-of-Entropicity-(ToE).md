# Challenges Before Obidi's Principle of Least Entropic Resistance (PoLER) of the Theory of Entropicity (ToE)

Challenges-Before-Obidi's-Principle-of-Least-Entropic-Resistance-(PoLER)-of-the-Theory-of-Entropicity-(ToE).md

Deriving classical dynamics from an independently defined entropic principle is a substantial foundational research problem. My suggestion to begin with the harmonic oscillator may have made the task sound easier than it is. The oscillator is a simple system to describe; deriving its dynamics from a new foundation can be extremely difficult.

The difficulty lies in establishing several connections simultaneously:

## Meaning:
What precisely is entropic resistance, and what physical quantity would measure it?

## Dynamics: 
Why should that resistance determine motion?

## Mathematics: 
Does the proposed optimization produce consistent, sufficiently well-defined equations?

## Correspondence: 
How do reversible motion, inertia, oscillation, and conservation emerge?

## Evidence: 
What would distinguish the entropic explanation from an alternative description of the same observations?


The deepest obstacle is that entropy alone generally does not specify the rate or route of evolution. A theory must explain how its entropic structure supplies that missing dynamical information. If the needed dynamics are inserted through assumptions, those assumptions must be acknowledged; otherwise, the derivation can inadvertently assume what it seeks to explain.

That makes it important to distinguish three achievements:

### * Conceiving PoLER is proposing a physical organizing principle. 

### * Formulating PoLER is giving that principle precise mathematical content. 

### * Establishing PoLER is demonstrating its consequences and testing whether they describe nature. 

These are different stages of a research program, and the first does not automatically accomplish the others.

The appropriate next objective is therefore narrower than deriving all mechanics: construct one explicit model in which entropic resistance is independently defined, explain the assumptions behind it, and determine what motion actually follows. Even a restricted result—or a clear demonstration that a particular formulation fails—would provide useful progress.

The general sense that the undertaking is formidable is accurate. 

* Its difficulty neither validates nor discredits PoLER.
* What matters is whether each step makes the proposal **more precise, less dependent on unstated assumptions, and more open to calculation and testing.**

# Scholium 


The critics making this objection are pointing out what is perhaps the most deep-seated obstacle to accepting John Onimisi Obidi's framework. In standard physics, their objection is 100% correct: traditionally, entropy is a macroscopic property of a system, not a localized force pushing an individual particle from point A to point B. [1] 

When critics say Obidi is wrong, they are usually leaning on three classic, heavyweight arguments against using entropy to define individual trajectories:

## 1. The Category Error (Statistical vs. Ontological)
In classical physics, an individual electron does not "have" entropy. Entropy only emerges when you group billions of electrons together and measure their collective disorder. Telling a single particle to obey an "entropic path" sounds to mainstream physicists like telling a single citizen to obey a "traffic statistic." Critics argue that Obidi is committing a category error by treating a statistical summary as if it were a physical force field.

## 2. The Arrow of Time Collision
If you throw a ball from point A to point B, classical mechanics says it traces a path that is perfectly reversible (ignoring air resistance). If you filmed it and ran the tape backward, it would follow the exact same path. Entropy, by definition, is one-way (irreversible). Critics argue that if a trajectory were governed by entropy, a particle could never trace its path backward, which contradicts centuries of precise, reversible orbital mechanics and particle tracking data.

## 3. The Definition of a "Point"
To move from point A to point B, those points must exist a priori in a blank background stage (spacetime). Standard thermodynamics requires space to already exist so that particles have a place to disperse. Critics argue you cannot use entropy to guide a particle through space if space itself isn't a fundamental given.

## How Obidi Defends Against the "Wrong" Label
Obidi's [Theory of Entropicity (ToE)](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6599558) handles these exact attacks by completely flipping what the word "entropy" means. He is not using the textbook definition of thermodynamic messiness. [1] 


### Matter is Not Moving "Through" Space: 
Obidi's most radical counter-argument—the Obidi Conjecture—proposes that space, time, and particles do not exist on their own. A particle is not an independent marble rolling from point A to point B. Instead, a particle is a localized "knot" or traffic jam in an underlying information network. [2, 3, 4] 

### Points are Entropic Events: 
In ToE, what we call "point A" and "point B" are not empty geometric locations; they are entropic events. The only reason point A is distinguishable from point B is due to gradients in a fundamental, continuous scalar entropy field, $S(x)$. [5, 6] 

### The Sensation of Motion: 
When a body moves from point A to point B, it isn't an object traveling through a vacuum. It is an informational pattern shifting across a network. Under the [Principle of Least Entropy Resistance (PoLER)](https://handwiki.org/wiki/Physics:Einstein%27s_Relativity_and_Obidi%27s_Theory_of_Entropicity%28ToE%29), the pattern shifts along the path that allows the underlying field to reorganize its information with the absolute highest efficiency. [1, 2, 7] 


## Why the Battle Persists
The critics are right to call it bizarre because, under traditional rules, it is impossible. Obidi's theory only works if you accept his premise that the physical universe is an illusion and information geometry is the true reality. Until ToE can propose a concrete experiment showing a physical divergence where classical mechanics fails and entropic resistance holds true, mainstream physics will continue to insist that entropy belongs to statistical systems, not individual moving bodies.
