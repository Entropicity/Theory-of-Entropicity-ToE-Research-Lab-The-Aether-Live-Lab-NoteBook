## 1. The Metric of Distance: Information Geometry
In standard physics, you use the Pythagorean metric $ds^2 = dx^2 + dy^2 + dz^2$ to measure the distance between two points in space. In Obidi's framework, physical distance is an emergent illusion generated by the Fisher Information Metric.

If Object A is represented by a probability distribution $P_A(\theta)$ and Object B by $P_B(\theta)$, the "distance" between them is determined by how much their statistical states overlap.

$\mathcal{I}_{ij}(\theta) = \sum_{k} P_k(\theta) \frac{\partial \ln P_k(\theta)}{\partial \theta^i} \frac{\partial \ln P_k(\theta)}{\partial \theta^j}$

* 
* **High Overlap (Correlated States):** The statistical difference is near zero. Physically, Object A and Object B are right next to each other (0 meters apart).
* **Low Overlap (Orthogonal States):** The statistical difference is high. Physically, Object A and Object B are far apart (5 meters apart).
* 

Therefore, saying "Object A is 5 meters away from Object B" is the macroscopic physical manifestation of saying "The quantum/informational states of Object A and Object B require a specific amount of entropic work to become statistically indistinguishable."

## 3. The "No-Rush" Limit of Space Generation
Your 5-meter gap (as a separation distance between object A and object B, for example) in physical spacetime is not "nothingness"; it is a buffer of intermediate informational states. For Object A to move 5 meters toward Object B, it must sequentially alter its probability states to match the intermediate states.

Because the universe cannot reorganize its information instantly (governed by the "No-Rush" Theorem), this rewriting process takes time. The speed of light ($c$) is simply the absolute cosmic bandwidth limit for how fast the entropic field can update its local information. [3, 4]
