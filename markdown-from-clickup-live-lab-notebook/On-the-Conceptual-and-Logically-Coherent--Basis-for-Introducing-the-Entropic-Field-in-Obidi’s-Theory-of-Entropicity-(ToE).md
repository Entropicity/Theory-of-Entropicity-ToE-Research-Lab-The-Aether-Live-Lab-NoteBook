# 🌌 Entropy Before Information, Before Spacetime:
Obidi’s Foundational Insight in the Theory of Entropicity (ToE)  

## A scholarly yet accessible exploration of why Obidi argues that entropy is the true substrate of physical reality

---

At the core of Obidi’s Theory of Entropicity (ToE) lies a strikingly original hypothesis: a physical point is not an empty geometric location but an entropic event — a locus of distinguishability generated by entropy itself. This reverses the conventional hierarchy of physics and information theory. Instead of treating entropy as a derivative statistical measure, Obidi argues that entropy is universal, ontologically prior, and the generator of information, spacetime, and matter.

This insight reshapes the conceptual foundations of physics. Hereunder, we provide a structured exposition of the logic behind Obidi’s proposal, written for readers seeking a deeper understanding of the entropic field $\(S(x)\)$ and its role in Obidi's radical and yet coherent Theory of Entropicity (ToE).

---

## 🔥 1. Entropy as the Universal Precursor of Information

In standard physics, information is treated as primary and entropy as a secondary quantity computed from informational states. Obidi challenges this view. Because entropy is universal — and because the second law of thermodynamics ensures its irreversibility and primacy — Obidi argues that entropy precedes information.

Information exists only because entropy creates distinguishability, gradients, and contrasts. Without entropy, there would be no differences to encode, no states to compare, and no informational content to extract.

Thus, entropy is not a statistical summary of information.  
Entropy is the precondition for information.

This leads to Obidi’s decisive claim:  
👉 Even what we call a “point” in spacetime is an entropic event.

We perceive spacetime because entropy makes points distinguishable.

## 🌐 2. Physical Points as Entropic–Informational Events

In conventional physics, an event \(x \in \mathcal{M}\) is a geometric abstraction. Obidi replaces this with a richer ontology: each event is an entropic–informational phenomenon.

A physical point carries:

- an entropic state S(x)  
- an informational state I(x)  
- a distinguishability structure generated by entropy  

Information is intrinsic, not epistemic. Entropy is fundamental, not emergent.  
The informational content of a point is a consequence of its entropic state.

## 📊 3. Information Admits an Entropic Representation

Shannon’s information theory shows that information and entropy are mathematically intertwined. For a probability distribution \(\{p_i\}\):

$$\[
H = -\sumi pi \log p_i
\]$$

and for an event of probability \(p\):

$$\[
I = -\log p.
\]$$

Obidi uses this to argue that if every point carries information, and if information is entropic in origin, then every point must possess a local entropic state.

This is the conceptual basis for introducing the entropic field \(S(x)\).

## 🔄 4. The Conceptual Reversal: Spacetime Emerges from Entropy

The traditional hierarchy is:

spacetime → matter → information → entropy

Obidi reverses it:

entropy → information → spacetime → matter

This reversal is the radical core of ToE.  
Spacetime does not contain entropy; spacetime arises from entropy.

Geometry is not fundamental.  
It is emergent from the entropic structure of events.

Distinguishability — created by entropy — is what allows spacetime to appear as a manifold of separable points.
