Einstein and Bohr Finally Reconciled by the Theory of Entropicity (ToE)

The paper **“Einstein and Bohr Finally Reconciled by the Theory of Entropicity (ToE)”**, by **John Onimisi Obidi**, was developed as a contribution to the **Centennial Celebration of Quantum Mechanics on 14 April 2025**.

Its central thesis was that ToE attempts to reconcile **Einstein’s insistence on objective reality and deeper causal structure** with **Bohr’s complementarity, contextuality, and irreversibility** by making **entropy the common physical denominator**. In that framework, quantum measurement and wave-function collapse are not treated merely as updates of knowledge, but as **physical, entropy-driven transitions**.

The paper’s key line of reasoning was that every observation requires an interaction and therefore an entropy transfer. The **Vuli-Ndlela Integral and associated inequality** were used to argue that a finite irreducible interaction interval is required before an observable can become physically definite. This was then connected to complementarity: position and momentum cannot both be fully actualized within the same interaction window because their entropic realization channels and collapse conditions are incompatible.

A particularly important part of the paper was its treatment of **wave-function collapse as an entropic phase transition**. Rather than taking collapse as an unexplained axiom, ToE proposed that a quantum system crosses an entropic threshold during interaction, after which one observable configuration becomes physically realized. The earlier formulation used a condition of the schematic form

$$
\frac{dS}{dt}\geq \Lambda_c,
$$

where the entropy-production rate reaches a critical threshold associated with definite observability.

The reconciliation was therefore not that Einstein or Bohr was simply “right” and the other “wrong.” The ToE position was that they were emphasizing different levels of the same physics. **Bohr correctly captured the operational, contextual, irreversible character of measurement**, while **Einstein’s demand for an underlying physical account remained legitimate**. ToE attempted to supply that deeper account through an entropic dynamics beneath the standard quantum description.

The paper also connected this interpretation to **EPR, entanglement, Bell-type nonlocal correlations, thermodynamics, information, causality, and relativity**. The broader claim was that Copenhagen quantum mechanics could retain its empirical success while ToE supplied a dynamical explanation for why measurement is irreversible, why definite outcomes emerge, and why observability itself has a finite entropic cost.

Importantly, the paper also used a relation of the form

$$
\Lambda=-k_B\ln|\Psi|^2
$$

in parts of the earlier framework, which is directly relevant to the entropic-probability correspondence discussed in another paper. That makes the subsequent paper on the Obidi probability-field relation highly connected to the Einstein–Bohr reconciliation paper rather than an isolated later idea.
