## ⚛️ 1. In Theoretical Physics: Shifting from Geometry to Processing Speed
For over a century, physics has treated the speed of light ($c$) as a cosmic magic trick—an unexplainable, hardcoded limit built into empty space. 

If you ask a classical physicist why light travels at exactly **$299,792,458\text{ m/s}$,** they cannot tell you; Einstein simply stated that it does. [3] 

## Obidi’s NRT changes this entirely:

### The Universe as a Substrate: 
The NRT treats the foundational "empty space" of the universe as a dense, underlying scalar field—the entropic manifold. [5, 6] 

### The "Stiffness" of Reality: 
This entropic field has an intrinsic processing limit, much like a computer’s central processor. It takes a finite amount of time for a change in one location to be calculated, synchronized, and registered in another. [3, 7, 8] 

### Demystifying $c$: 
The speed of light is not a property of light at all. It is the maximum processing throughput of the universal entropic field. A photon travels at $c$ simply because it has zero mass, meaning it experiences no resistance and moves at the maximum rendering speed the universe allows. [3] 


By shifting the speed limit from an arbitrary geometric rule to a derived thermodynamic constraint, the NRT provides a deep, physical explanation for why relativity exists. [9, 10]

The greatest crisis in modern physics is that General Relativity (which assumes a smooth, continuous spacetime geometric canvas) completely contradicts Quantum Mechanics (which relies on jumpy, probabilistic wavefunctions).
The NRT bridges this chasm by redefining how physical properties emerge:

Relativity views motion as a smooth, continuous line through a spacetime grid. The NRT states that motion is actually an it-from-bit sequence of step-by-step entropic calculations. Space and time only appear smooth to us because the individual calculation steps happen too fast for our macroscopic eyes to see. [2, 3]

In quantum mechanics, when a particle is observed, its probability wave collapses into a definite location seemingly instantly. The NRT mathematically forbids this "instantaneity". Instead, it shows that what we call "collapse" is a rapid, finite-time entropic stabilization process. The wave function hits an entropic boundary condition and takes a highly compressed—but non-zero—fraction of a second to settle into a physical state. [4, 11, 12]

   ### 2. Heraclitean Process: 
   The belief that there are no permanent "things," and that change, flow, and transformation are the only true realities.

Obidi’s NRT provides a mathematical victory for process philosophy. It establishes an entirely new branch of philosophical inquiry called Ontodynamics—the physics of existence as entropic motion. [12]
