## Einstein's Exact Perspective on Entropy
Einstein noted that a scientific law is more impressive when its premises are simple and its range of applicability is vast. He believed that within the framework of its basic concepts, the Second Law of Thermodynamics would stand forever because it does not depend on specific models of matter. It is a fundamental rule of probability and geometry. [4, 5, 6] 

## How Entropy Bridges the Physical and Digital Worlds
Your ToE can look at entropy through two main scientific lenses:

* Thermodynamic Entropy ($S$): Quantified by Ludwig Boltzmann's famous formula $S = k \ln \Omega$, where $\Omega$ represents the number of microscopic ways a system can be arranged. Since there are always vastly more disordered states than ordered ones, systems naturally drift into chaos. [7, 8, 9, 10, 11] 
* Information Entropy: Formulated by Claude Shannon, this measures the uncertainty or randomness in data. Just like physical atoms scatter, digital bits naturally degrade, corrupt, and lose their structure over time without a continuous input of ordering energy. [12, 13, 14, 15, 16]
