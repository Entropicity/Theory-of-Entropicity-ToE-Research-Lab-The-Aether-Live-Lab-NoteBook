The **Theory of Entropicity (ToE)**, along with the **Obidi index convention,** represents a highly radical, audacious and emerging conceptual framework proposed by researcher **John Onimisi Obidi.** [1, 2] 

Rather than viewing entropy as a passive, secondary measurement of statistical disorder (as traditional thermodynamics does), this framework flips the script: it elevates entropy to a primary, continuous physical field $S(x)$ from which spacetime, matter, and gravity fundamentally emerge. [3, 4] 
## Key Conceptual Shifts in This Framework


* Spacetime as an Illusion: Spacetime is not an empty background box where things happen. It is a coarse-grained illusion arising from a deeper "entropic manifold". [2, 5] 
* Gravity as a Projection: Following the lineage of emergent gravity theorists like Erik Verlinde, this framework posits that if information geometry has statistical metrics, and entropy is built on those distributions, entropy itself has a geometry—and that geometry is what we perceive as gravitational curvature. [6, 7] 
* The Speed of Light Reinterpreted: Instead of treating $c$ as an arbitrary, primitive constant input, the theory's No-Rush Theorem derives $c$ as the maximum physical rate at which the universal entropic field can rearrange information and energy. [3, 8] 
* Einstein–Obidi Synthesis: The index convention acts as the mathematical bridge, forcing standard Einstein tensor calculus to explicitly account for underlying statistical and quantum geometric sectors (like Fisher–Rao and Fubini–Study) simultaneously. [1] 
  

## Current Scientific Status
While it builds elegantly on established academic branches like information geometry, thermodynamics, and emergent gravity research, it is important to note that the Theory of Entropicity (ToE) is currently an emerging audacious and provocative alternative framework primarily documented in published monographs, preprints, and essays. 

It is still undergoing mainstream peer-review and yet to engage widespread experimental validation required to make it stand alongside established models like General Relativity or the Standard Model. [1, 7, 9, 10]
