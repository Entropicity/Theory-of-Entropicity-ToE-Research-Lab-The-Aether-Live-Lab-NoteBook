# The Owolawi Existential Constraint (OEC) — also called the Owolawi Logical Dilemma (OLD) or Owolawi Existential Dilemma (OED) - in the Owolawi-Obidi-Correspondence (OOC)

The Owolawi Existential Constraint (OEC) — also called the Owolawi Logical Dilemma (OLD) or Owolawi Existential Dilemma (OED) — is one of the most elegant and troubling formulations to emerge from the Owolawi–Obidi-Correspondence (OOC). It begins with a simple question — “Who created the creator?” — but ends by destabilizing the very foundations of metaphysics, theology, duality, and human moral experience.

---

The Owolawi Existential Constraint (OEC): A Comprehensive Philosophical Exposition

1. The Primordial Question: Who Created the Creator?
The dilemma begins with a familiar theological provocation:

> “A guy named Norm said there has to be a creator. Then Pamela Smyth asks who created the creator?” 

This question, ancient yet ever‑renewed, forces metaphysics into a regress:  
If everything has a cause, then the creator must also have a cause.  
If the creator has no cause, then the principle of causality is violated.

Owolawi’s intervention breaks this regress by introducing a dualistic ontology grounded in polar opposites.

---

2. Owolawi’s Foundational Insight: Nothingness Requires Its Opposite
Owolawi argues that nothingness cannot exist alone. It requires a complementary opposite — somethingness — in order to be conceptually meaningful:

> “For absolutely nothing to exist, it also needs a polar opposite which is something… nothingness itself can never exist on its own without its polar opposite.” 

This is the core of the Owolawi Existential Constraint:

OEC Principle 1 — Ontological Duality
If any ontological state exists, its opposite must exist simultaneously.  
Nothingness → requires → Somethingness  
Absence → requires → Presence  
Void → requires → Creator

Thus, the creator is not “created.”  
The creator is the polar opposite of nothingness, and both must coexist eternally.

This is not theology.  
This is logical necessity.

---

3. Obidi’s Expansion: Duality Makes God’s Existence Tautological
Obidi immediately recognizes the profundity of this structure:

> “Once we accept… that things exist in opposites, then it is pointless to want to know about the existence of God, because God’s existence is therefore inclusive and hence tautological!” 

If duality is fundamental, then:

- If nothingness exists, somethingness must exist.  
- If somethingness exists, a creator must exist.  
- Therefore, God’s existence is not optional — it is structurally required.

This is the Owolawi–Obidi Theorem of Necessary Duality.

---

4. The Second Regression: Who Created Duality Itself?
Obidi then pushes the argument into deeper waters:

> “Who or what initiated the existence of the opposites or dualities we experience? Why must such opposites exist in the first place?” 

This is the Owolawi Logical Dilemma:

OEC Principle 2 — The Duality Origin Problem
If duality is necessary for existence,  
and existence requires duality,  
then what caused duality to exist?

This question collapses back into the original theological dilemma, forming a closed existential loop:

Creator → Opposites → Creator → Opposites → …

Thus, the OEC reveals that the question “Who created the creator?” is ill‑posed, because it assumes a linear causal chain rather than a dualistic ontological structure.

---

5. The Moral Catastrophe: The Good–Evil Entanglement
Owolawi then introduces the most disturbing consequence of his logic:

> “Can there be death without life? Can there be good if there was no evil?” 

Obidi recognizes the existential danger immediately:

> “Does it not mean that the moment we talk of Good, we automatically call in the very elements of Evil… whenever we do good, don’t we also automatically bring forth evil?” 

This is the Owolawi Existential Constraint in its most troubling form:

OEC Principle 3 — Moral Entanglement
Every moral action automatically generates its opposite.  
Good → calls forth → Evil  
Evil → calls forth → Good

This is not metaphor.  
It is a logical consequence of duality.

Obidi notes that this destroys the Buddhist Middle Way and collapses moral philosophy into a quantum entanglement of ethical opposites.

---

6. The Kybalion Connection: The Principle of Polarity
Obidi identifies that Owolawi’s logic mirrors the Fourth Hermetic Principle:

> “Everything is dual; everything has poles… Opposites are identical in nature but different in degree.” 

This places the OEC within a lineage of esoteric metaphysics, but Owolawi’s formulation is sharper, more rigorous, and more dangerous, because it applies duality not only to metaphysical states but to moral actions and cosmic origins.

---

7. The Final Consequence: Human Existence in a Dire Strait
Obidi summarizes the existential weight of Owolawi’s discovery:

> “Has your logic not now entangled us all in a distressing bind… that our good calls forth evil and our evil calls forth good?” 

This is the Owolawi Existential Dilemma:

OEC Principle 4 — The Inescapability of Opposites
Human beings cannot escape duality.  
Every action, thought, or designation creates its opposite.  
Existence is a perpetual entanglement.

This is why Obidi concludes:

> “I am only bedazzled at the consequences… we are now living in a dire strait.” 

---

8. The Philosophical Significance of the OEC
The Owolawi Existential Constraint accomplishes four unprecedented things:

1). It resolves the “creator regress” without invoking theology.
Duality makes the creator logically necessary.

2). It reframes nothingness as an ontological impossibility.
Nothingness cannot exist without somethingness.

3). It introduces a moral entanglement principle.
Good and evil are co‑generated, not independent.

4). It exposes the existential instability of human meaning.
Every designation creates its opposite, destabilizing all absolutes.

This is why Obidi calls it:

> “One of the most significant expositions in the Owolawi–Obidi-Correspondence (OOC).” 

---

9. Final Synthesis: The Owolawi–Obidi Ontological Framework
The OEC can be summarized as a four‑step ontological structure:

1. Duality is fundamental.  
2. Nothingness requires somethingness.  
3. Somethingness is the creator.  
4. Duality itself is uncreated and necessary.

Thus:

- The creator is uncreated.  
- Nothingness is uncreated.  
- Both coexist as polar opposites.  
- All existence emerges from their tension.  
- All moral states emerge from their entanglement.

This is the Owolawi Logical Dilemma:  
To exist is to be trapped in duality.

This is the Owolawi Existential Constraint:  
To act is to generate your opposite.

This is the Owolawi Existential Dilemma:  
To seek the creator is to rediscover nothingness.
