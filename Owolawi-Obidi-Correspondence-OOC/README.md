# Some Highly Pertinent and Necessary Historical Briefs for Posterity 

# Michele Angelo Besso (1873–1955)

* **Who he was:** A Swiss-Italian mechanical engineer whom Einstein met during their student days at the Federal Polytechnic Institute in Zurich.

* **Role/Contribution:** Besso worked with Einstein at the Swiss Patent Office in Bern and served as a vital intellectual "sounding board". Einstein famously thanked Besso by name at the end of his pioneering 1905 special relativity paper.

**Historically, the man Besso was the only "reference and credit" to grace that Einstein revolutionary and epochal paper of his Annus Mirabilis: On the Electrodynamics of Moving Bodies.**


-----------


# Heinrich Zangger (1874–1957)

* **Who he was:** A Swiss professor of forensic medicine and director of the Institute of Forensic Medicine at the University of Zurich.

* **Role/Contribution:** Zangger was a deeply trusted confidant and pillar of emotional and professional support. During the intense years Einstein was finalizing his general theory of relativity, he maintained a rich and detailed scientific and personal correspondence with Zangger.

# [Marcel Grossmann](https://www.google.com/search?q=marcel+grossmann&kgmid=/m/04lwdv#sv=CBwS8wMKugMStwMK9wJBSmlUNHRJS2FXVDNCbVk4X3ZydEdNUDlnV2NqYUtacjJfYTVlME1KNlhEamcyR3JhME1GX2FPWWVkNHNWbTFneXJFOVBRUFdQbm01WDJ5TWc3NEt5ODNpTW0wQm1TZUFaTFVJbmlmM1J4a3h2OUJFajBFNXJtSXdSSWljME5YMVpDaXlrYk50UHdxYTZOcjlqNVdjT2NvWjhkeGFWYjBGdGtTd18yajZnRVF5MWFjcEN2VWgteW1zUkVFYjRxUXlUcWI0RzBKZUs0WXBaQW9FTWh2VEtKM3pySEFUSG9uZzhNbktRNGw2dU9uNEd0OVFUbTFWejd4YXBoMHBXSy1GQUJaQ2d6SEI4blNzMXNpdXlzRmgzS1g1RGlSdlVzYTJNOTVsNThWNzhTSUgxc1BKYk42aHF4UUF1WmxPdU5xWXJGVWRmejA5eTUzV0NlTUFnaEpkbWJRSjNkaFNZeDdLbVNteHhkeGV6WkptaW9lOGl4Qko4SzQSF2RlOWhhcWJaTDRURHJ1RVAwWkt4cVFzGiJBRHNyOWZRcV9HX1M5eExUMTV6cHJPcEZ0OVNEOWhJR09BEgQ3ODU0GgEzIhUKAXESEG1hcmNlbCBncm9zc21hbm4iEgoFa2dtaWQSCS9tLzA0bHdkdigAGEUgl5j_vAg) (1878–1936) 
Marcel Grossman was a brilliant Swiss mathematician, an ETH Zurich classmate, and one of Albert Einstein's most critical lifelines in both his early career and the formulation of the [General Theory of Relativity](https://www.britannica.com/story/how-albert-einstein-developed-the-theory-of-general-relativity). [1, 2] 

* **The Math Savior:** While Einstein possessed unmatched physical intuition, he lacked the advanced mathematical tools needed to model a curved four-dimensional universe. He famously begged Grossmann, "Grossmann, you must help me or else I'll go crazy!". Grossmann introduced Einstein to non-Euclidean geometry, [Riemannian geometry](https://www.aps.org/publications/apsnews/200505/history.cfm), and tensor calculus, which became the structural math foundation for General Relativity. [3, 4, 5] 
* **Co-Authoring the Blueprint:** They collaborated closely to publish the Entwurf ("Outline") paper in 1913. This was the essential mathematical precursor that put Einstein on the direct path to his final 1915 gravitational field equations of Einstein's Masterpiece (or "coup de grâce"), General Relativity. [1, 3, 6, 7] 
* **Early Career Lifesaver:** Even before their physics collaboration, Grossmann saved Einstein twice. First, Einstein frequently skipped university math lectures, so he passed his exams by studying Grossmann’s meticulously kept notebook. Second, when Einstein was unemployed and struggling after graduation, Grossmann's father used family connections to help Einstein secure his legendary job at the [Swiss Patent Office](https://en.ara.cat/science-technology/the-man-who-taught-mathematics-at-einstein_130_5586297.html). [8, 9, 10]
